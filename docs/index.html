How to read this

Three passes, each narrowing the last. The fundalist scored all 207 tickers in today’s business overview and passed 148. The technicalist re-derived a fresh read for those from the Dual_X grid — but Dual_X only covered 37 names today, so only 29 of the 148 got a technical read at all and 19 cleared. The sentilist then rated the bulletin, keeping 6+, and separately scanned every one of the 203 tickers in the bulletin so a late catalyst can surface a name the funnel never saw.

Credit spreads are the default because the wall beat the direction. In the 29/08–11/09 review, price held above the put wall 69% of the time against 48% for a directional read, and stayed below the call wall 85% against 58%. So the short strike sits on the dealer wall and the exit is a close through it. A trade is skipped rather than reshaped: if the wall strike does not pay ⅓ of the width, the answer is no trade, never a strike moved closer to price.

The six gates, applied in order, first failure decides. G1 a bull trade needs fundamentals 7+ (the group below won 22%); bear trades have no floor. G2 sentiment 8+ against fundamentals under 8.5, or a headline reporting a confirmed large move — the news is already in the price (12%). G3 a run-up over 8% in five days in the trade’s own direction is a chase (29%) — wait for the named level. G4 a bull lean below the put wall, or in Mark-down/Distribution (30%/34%). G5 direction bias, sentiment and Wyckoff disagreeing (39%) — a conflict is now a pass, not a smaller trade. G6 no wall, no short strike: sentiment-only rows and fundamentals-plus-news rows with no technical read go to the watch list.

Cushion vs 7-day implied. The cushion is the distance from price to the short strike. When it is smaller than the 7-day implied move it is flagged THIN CUSHION — the market is pricing a one-week move that reaches the strike, so the wall has to do real work.
