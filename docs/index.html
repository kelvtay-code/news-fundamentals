Credit spreads are the default because the wall beat the direction. In the 29/08–11/09 review, price held above the put wall 69% of the time against 48% for a directional read. It stayed below the call wall 85% of the time against 58%. So the short strike sits on the dealer wall, and the exit is a close through it. If the wall strike doesn’t pay ⅓ of the width, there is no trade. The strike is never moved closer to price.

The six gates run in order, and the first failure decides. G1: a bull trade needs fundamentals of 7+ (the group below won 22%). Bear trades have no floor. G2: sentiment 8+ against fundamentals under 8.5, or a headline reporting a confirmed large move, means the news is already in the price (12%). G3: a run-up of more than 8% in five sessions in the trade’s own direction is a chase (29%). Wait for the named level. G4: a bull lean below the put wall, or in Mark-down/Distribution (30%/34%). G5: direction bias, sentiment and Wyckoff disagree (39%). A conflict is now a pass, not a smaller trade. G6: no wall, no short strike. Sentiment-only rows and fundamentals-plus-news rows with no technical read go to the watch list.

Cushion vs 7-day implied. The cushion is the distance from price to the short strike. When it is smaller than the 7-day implied move, it is flagged THIN CUSHION: the market is pricing a one-week move that reaches the strike.
