How to read this

Fundalist scored all 204 tickers in the latest business_overview_*.csv on a deterministic 1-10 formula (analyst rating, upside to target, PEG, forward P/E, revenue growth, profit margin) and passed forward 174 names scoring ≥5 -- not shown in full here, only the ones that matter to the funnel below.

Technicalist worked only the 17 names in the latest Dual_X co-expansion screen (both IV slope and GEX slope positive over the snapshot window -- this is the "dual expand"), cross-checked against the broader watchlist screen for a live volume-surge ratio (the closest available proxy to a 5-day vs 30-day volume read; no snapshot carries a literal 5d/30d average). 12 of 15 fundalist-passed names cleared ≥5 (APH, AAPL and MOD did not); SMR, XLY and XLB were already out at Fundalist (4.8, 3.7, 3.7).

Sentilist read the actual headlines for each surviving ticker from the latest news bulletin and rated trade conviction 1-10 -- how real and specific the catalyst is, not just its tone. A strong, specific negative catalyst (a downgrade, a safety probe) scores as high as a strong positive one; the arrow (▲/▼) next to the score carries the direction, and the trade proposal follows the arrow, not just the score.

The 7+ catch-all is mandatory and independent of the funnel: it scanned all 752 headline cards across all 192 covered tickers using a catalyst-keyword pre-filter (earnings beats/misses, upgrades/ downgrades, FDA approvals, M&A, guidance changes, etc.), then read the ~48 flagged candidates directly to rate the 10 that were both genuinely ticker-specific and rated 7 or higher. This same scan is what caught the D/NextEra merger (see Tier 1) -- proof this pass earns its keep, not just a formality. This is a pre-filtered scan, not an exhaustive hand-read of all 192 tickers -- a real catalyst below the keyword net could exist but wasn't caught.

Conflicts are flagged, not hidden. Where Wyckoff phase, GEX regime, or wall position disagree with the news direction (AMZN, SYK, CVS, PLTR, NET, HD, MRVL, SANM, WDAY), the trade is sized down or dropped to a watch-only call rather than forced -- see each card's amber conflict note.

Data quality flag: two ticker-tag collisions this run. Every "PINS" headline was actually about Meta, not Pinterest -- PINS was dropped rather than scored on bad data. And the Dominion/NextEra merger story ran entirely under the "NEE" tag, never under "D" -- caught only by the wider catch-all scan, which is exactly what that scan is for.
