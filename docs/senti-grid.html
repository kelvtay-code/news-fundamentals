Technology is half the board — but it is not one trade

35 of the 73 names are Technology, and the instinct is to read that as a single AI signal. It isn't. The cluster splits cleanly: AI infrastructure and optics (LITE, CIEN, CLS, AAOI, NOK, JBL, APH, FN-adjacent names) moved on the Lumentum-sparked optics rally; memory and storage (MU, STX, WDC, DELL, DRAM) moved on the AI server and storage cycle; quantum (QNT, INFQ, IONQ, RGTI) moved on post-IPO earnings and two Trump executive orders. Three separate catalysts, not one.

The single largest mover on this grid — WDAY at 9 — is unrelated to any of them: it is a Silver Lake buyout negotiation worth $8.6B in added deal value.

Three cautions before trading this list

1. This was a strong tape. The bulletin spans a session in which the S&P clocked its best weekly rally since 2023 and the Dow hit a record. A high count of bullish names partly reflects beta, not stock selection. Names that fell in that tape — CSCO (slid 8% despite an earnings beat), COHR (dropped despite a blowout Q4), QCOM, HD — carry more company-specific information than most of the winners.

2. Michael Burry is short four names that appear or nearly appear here: NBIS (7 on this grid), MU (7), ORCL and PLTR. A stock can be rallying on headlines and be a crowded short simultaneously; the senti score measures the former only.

3. Insider selling sits inside several bullish scores — AAOI's CFO, WDC's CEO, STX's Form 144, CRWD's CEO (which is part of why CrowdStrike scored 5 and is absent here). Read the brief, not just the number.
