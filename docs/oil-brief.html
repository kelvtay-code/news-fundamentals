🌍 The Hormuz De-escalation Trade
HEADLINE >> PHYSICAL

What is genuinely de-escalating

  • Iran and Oman agreed Aug 25 to a "phased framework" temporary navigational corridor — a "two-lane highway" — plus mine clearance. Once the route is finalised, 30–60 days to negotiate a permanent transit map.
  • US Secretary of State Rubio told counterparts the US is not expected to initiate new strikes on Iran "for the time being" (Axios, Aug 26).
  • The US shifted to a targeted economic approach — Aug 24 sanctions on 60 entities/individuals/vessels — which markets read as sanctions-instead-of-bombs.
  • Pakistani officials, post-Tehran visit, report "significant progress" on ending the war (ING note, Aug 26).

What has NOT changed on the water

  • 8 ships transited Hormuz on Aug 25 (revised up from 7 on Aug 24) — a mix of compliant and Iran-linked. That is a chokepoint still functionally closed.
  • Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority published a blacklist Aug 23 threatening "fines, seizure, or confiscation" of non-compliant ships.
  • Iranian naval vessels intercepted and turned back an Indian tanker attempting to enter the Gulf on Aug 26.
  • VLGC Royal H reported "not under command" in the Gulf of Oman a day past ETA — operational risk, not just legal risk.
  • No timetable was attached to the Iran–Oman framework by either side.
Analyst read. This is an asymmetric setup. The flat-price move on Aug 26 (Brent -74c, WTI -13c, Asian crude down >2% intraday) discounted a re-opening that has produced exactly one incremental transit. Meanwhile every physical tightness metric that the closure created — Dubai trading above Brent, Fujairah residual stocks -31.7%, VLCC index at $525k/day, 350 LR2s pulled into dirty trades — is still fully intact. Sell flat-price rallies on peace headlines only if you are simultaneously long the cracks and the sour structure those headlines cannot fix in a fortnight.
🇩🇪 Supply-Side Geopolitics — The Structural Hole in Refining
SUPPORTS DISTILLATE
  • Russia: Ukrainian military struck the Norsi refinery Aug 26 (fire reported; 40+ drones over Nizhny Novgorod). Perm refinery suspended operations after an Aug 21 strike and pulled sales off the St Petersburg exchange. BIMCO notes Russian export bans on clean products.
  • Global throughput loss: IEA estimates 4.7 mn b/d of lost throughput vs 2025; ClearView's Kevin Book puts missing product supply at 2–3 mn b/d. "There's no US-only solution to this."
  • Kazakhstan: 2026 target cut again to 96 mn mt (from 98 mn, and vs 99.56 mn in 2025) after CPC drone strikes. July output 1.55 mn b/d vs a 2026 high of 1.85 mn b/d in April. Druzhba exports zero for the next three months per Transneft plans.
  • Saudi rerouting: Yanbu exports cut from 3.8 mn b/d to 3 mn b/d (at times below 2 mn) by the Houthi embargo on Saudi-linked ships since July 20. Asia's share of those barrels fell from 80% to ~25%; over half now moves via Sumed to Sidi Kerir. This is a direct structural bullish input for Med/NWE sour supply and a bearish one for Asian sour availability.
  • Georgia/Kulevi: Black Sea Petroleum stopped refining Russian crude Aug 24, switching to Kazakh (rail) and Libyan (86,000 b/d in the week from Aug 17) barrels to escape the EU's 21st package.
  • Libya: output up to 1.4 mn b/d (from ~500,000 b/d in Sep-2024), targeting 2 mn b/d by decade-end; Es Sider last at -10c to Dated Brent. Greenstream running 1 Bcm/y against 11 Bcm/y capacity — an under-used European supply option.
  • US policy is out of levers: refineries already at 97.4%. EPA's Aug 20 winter-gasoline waiver (Sep 1 vs Sep 15) and extended Jones Act waivers (Aug 10, +90 days) are incremental. Export bans described as "extreme/unlikely end" of the spectrum by CERA's O'Neil.
🚨 Watch: Aug 26 US diesel and gasoline prices fell with RINs as refiners weighed a possible EPA decision to relax blending mandates and widen small-refinery exemptions. A permissive SRE/RVO outcome is a genuine near-term bearish input for USGC product cracks that has nothing to do with the Middle East.
