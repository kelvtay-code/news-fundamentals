3  |  Geopolitics — What Moved, and What It Prices
The conflict widened on two fronts and lengthened in time. The Houthis took a Red Sea port, Washington is reportedly discussing a war into 2029, and tankers now cross Hormuz with their transponders off. Oil flow through the Strait, as a result, can no longer be counted reliably.
🚨 Mokha, and a war with no end date
BULLISH duration
  • Yemeni forces seized the Red Sea port city of Mokha during US trading hours on Sept 10. That raised fears for Bab al-Mandab and Hormuz at once. Bab al-Mandab traffic fell to 36 ships from 39, and no Saudi-linked tankers used the strait on Sept 9.
  • The Wall Street Journal reported that White House advisers have discussed the US–Iran war stretching into 2029. Global Risk Management: the focus has shifted “towards the risk of a prolonged conflict where the Strait of Hormuz is ‘closed for longer’.”
  • ANZ’s Daniel Hynes: “The tit-for-tat attacks suggest oil flows from the Persian Gulf are likely to remain disrupted for the foreseeable future.”
  • Saudi exports are already rerouting. August crude loadings were 5.6 mn b/d, the lowest since May: 2.8 mn b/d from Yanbu, only 571,500 b/d from Ras Tanura, and 1.9 mn b/d from Sidi Kerir in Egypt, more than double July. SUMED pipeline flows hit 1.25 mn b/d, the highest since April 2020.
  • Saudi crude for Asia now leaves via the Mediterranean: 629,000 b/d loaded at Sidi Kerir for China, Korea and Japan in August. Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority had already blacklisted Saudi tankers from Hormuz on Aug 23.
Price read. Mokha takes the Red Sea bypass risk from theoretical to physical. Saudi Arabia’s workaround for Hormuz runs through the Red Sea to Yanbu and on to Suez, and that route now has a hostile force holding a port on its eastern shore. The market responded by bidding up products, Europe’s most of all, and Europe sits at the end of that route.
🛡️ Hormuz goes dark — APPEC, day four
UNMEASURABLE
  • Repsol’s Max Tay called the Strait “a mysterious black box.” Tankers are switching off AIS, painting over names and fitting anti-drone defences. Estimates of oil through the Strait range from 6 mn to 10 mn b/d. That $4 mn b/d range covers almost any view of the balance.
  • Emarat Maritime’s Rishi Nyati: ships on the southern Omani corridor are “completely dark”, with drone cages around bridges, electronic jamming and crew safe rooms. “I have been doing this for 30 years. I have never seen anything like this before.” Emarat has pulled its ships from the region.
  • S&P’s Rahul Kapoor: freight is up “primarily because of vessel supply, not cargo demand.” He expects normal transit to return only gradually, through end-2027.
  • MOL’s Takeshi Hashimoto: shipyard slots are almost fully booked, so a VLCC ordered today delivers in 4–5 years. MOL is replacing ships, not adding to its fleet. Owners of 10- to 20-year-old tankers can sell them at a high premium.
  • Counted transits: 17 on Sept 9, steady. Of 17, five were product tankers. The damaged New Andros is circling outside Hormuz with 1.9 mn bbl of Iraqi fuel oil.
⚠️ WHAT THIS DOES TO MY FREIGHT TRIGGER. My dirty-freight re-entry depends partly on counted Hormuz transits falling below 15. If tankers are transiting dark, the counted number is a floor, not a measurement. The trigger’s transit condition is now suspect. Method. Each call treats flat price, cracks, time spreads, cash differentials and freight separately, then checks each against three independent evidence types: MOC price formation, realised tender and sale prices over time, and physical logistics. When those disagree, the call is split by leg, sized down on both legs, or declined; conflicting signals are never averaged. Eleven disciplines are in force, each added after it cost money: (1) name the invalidation on the instrument actually traded; (2) a completed sale realisation outranks the shape of the forward curve; (3) one session in a direction is not a signal, in either direction; (4) every top-five position must cite a dated physical event, a completed realisation or a named counterparty action, never price action alone; (5) a single dated event buys one notch and a mid-table ranking, never two notches, a top-three slot or a new position at size; (6) mark the print, not the memory; (7) write invalidations as a single sufficient condition or two joined by OR, never AND, and where a thesis concerns a whole complex, name the complex rather than one operator’s units; (8) do not cut a position because of the order book when the differential itself has not moved against you: a thin book is a liquidity observation, not a directional signal; (9) write the invalidation in the same unit as the position: a freight position stops on a freight level, never on a proxy variable; (10) mark from the assessment table dated to the session, never from the commentary: the commentary quotes the previous close (replaces the withdrawn “mis-levelled stop” rule, whose premise was a misread print); (11) before pricing an outage, verify the asset was running.
