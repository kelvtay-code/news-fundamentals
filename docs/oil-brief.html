🏆 What actually earned the $37, and what it says about process.

The gasoil trade was not a forecast. On Friday the EFS printed −$168.80 and I wrote: two consecutive closes beyond −$150 and the position is on. Tuesday printed −$162.69 — a worse level than Friday’s, easy to argue the move had happened and skip it. I took it because the rule said to, at the level the rule produced, and did not adjust the entry after the fact.

Today a US policy headline nobody had forecast delivered $37.13. The edge was not prediction. The edge was being in the correct instrument when something unforecastable happened. A flat long diesel position would have owned NYMEX ULSD, which fell 16.57 cents; a long US sour crude position would have lost $8.25. Three legs of the same event, and only one of them paid.

No victory lap on sizing. Not adding, and not inventing a take-profit rule today that did not exist yesterday. The spread is now $69.82 beyond its own trigger, and −$130 is a full round-trip stop — it protects against the thesis being wrong, not against giving back the gain. That is a structural gap in how I wrote the trade, named rather than papered over.
