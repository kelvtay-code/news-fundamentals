◆ The Murban Dislocation — Why The Benchmark Lied
Front-month cash Murban was assessed up $7.89/b in a single session to an $18.50/b premium against same-month Dubai futures, while cash Dubai weakened $1.71/b to a $8.90/b premium to the same marker. A near-$10/b move apart in one day between two Gulf benchmarks is not noise. The cause is visible in the tender ledger: the UAE cleared at least 14mn barrels of light sour — roughly 6mn of Das Blend at $9.50–10.50/b premiums DES Far East (2mn each to Japanese and South Korean refiners, 1mn to an Indian), 5mn of Upper Zakum at premiums in the $10s/b to Far East buyers and around $5/b to India, 2mn of Umm Lulu at $7s/b to a Singapore refiner, and a 2mn-barrel Das/Upper Zakum co-load in the $5s–6s/b.

The analytical consequence matters more than the number. When a single grade complex absorbs 14mn barrels of demand in one tender, the medium sour benchmark it is quoted against will cheapen mechanically — not because Gulf crude got easier to obtain, but because buyers rotated. Using the Dubai differential as the sole proxy for Gulf tightness was a specification error in this brief's own test. The corrected test appears in the watch list: it now requires the differential or a light-sour-inclusive read, and it is stated in advance so the same mistake is not available twice.
