Macro Alert — Crude Craters >5% on Hormuz Deal Talk While the Strait Itself Says Otherwise; Products Refuse to Follow Crude Down

Today's tape is a textbook headline-vs-logistics divergence. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CNBC a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz "could come today or tomorrow" — and crude was hammered: September WTI settled −$4.57 (−5.7%) to $75.77/b and October Brent −$4.41 (−5.3%) to $79.36/b, the sharpest one-day drop since the conflict began. But the transit data moved the wrong way for a deal that's actually landed: Hormuz crossings fell to 15 ships on Aug 3 (from 19 the day before, 8 of the 15 Iran-linked), and Bab al-Mandab crossings collapsed to 21 ships — the lowest since June 1 — as the Houthi "maritime embargo" declared July 20 keeps biting (only 3 of the 21 were Saudi-linked). A third ship incident in four days was reported near Khasab, Oman late Aug 3. Physical crude quietly disagreed with the flat-price crash too: cash Dubai firmed $4.69/b to $82.90 and its premium to Dubai futures widened $3.45/b to $7.49/b — a backwardation signal that does not belong on a session where the market is meant to be pricing supply normalization. Underneath, the product complex simply refused to follow crude lower. Singapore 380cst HSFO rose +$19.54/mt against a collapsing crude tape, its Aug-Sep backwardation widening to $23.25/mt and its Hi-5 spread (LSFO vs HSFO) hitting $225.93/mt — the widest since November 2023. Marine Fuel 0.5% cash premiums, Singapore fuel oil MOC volumes (+35% MoM to a 5-month high) and the Arab Gulf naphtha inside/outside-Hormuz split (still ~$218.50/mt, essentially unmoved) all say the physical shortage this desk has tracked since late July has not actually resolved — only the crude futures screen has decided to believe the headline.

Product Analysis

Crude Oil — Split Call, Downgraded
Brent $79.36/b (−$4.41) · WTI $75.77/b (−$4.57) · Oct Dubai $82.90/b (+$4.69)
Bearish (flat price) Bullish — physical structure
The Headline That Broke the Tape
Bessent said a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz "could come today or tomorrow," triggering the sharpest single-session decline of the conflict — WTI −5.7%, Brent −5.3%. ING's Warren Patterson called the sell-off "fairly overdone... we've been in this situation multiple times before, only to see things unravel," and Iran denied direct talks were underway, saying it was working only with Oman on shipping access.
The Strait Itself Disagreed
Hormuz crossings fell to 15 ships on Aug 3 from 19 the day before (8 of 15 Iran-linked); Bab al-Mandab fell to 21 from 36, the lowest since June 1, with only 3 of 21 Saudi-linked as the Houthi "maritime embargo" (declared July 20) continues to bite. A third ship incident near Khasab, Oman in four days was reported at 2200 GMT Aug 3 — the Minoan Pioneer hit by an unidentified projectile.
Physical Crude Firmed While Futures Cratered
Cash Dubai rose $4.69/b to $82.90 and its premium to Dubai futures widened $3.45/b to $7.49/b — a genuine backwardation signal, the opposite of what a resolving-shortage narrative should produce. Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said Hormuz flows sit at "one-tenth of pre-conflict levels," the conflict has withheld 2.6bn barrels of supply, and the world needs 2.1mn b/d of extra crude over 18 months just to rebuild stocks once transit normalizes.
WTI/Brent −5.3% to −5.7% on Deal Headline Cash Dubai +$4.69/b, Backwardation +$3.45/b Hormuz 19→15, Bab al-Mandab 36→21 (Lowest Since Jun 1) Diamondback Raises 2026 US Guidance to 522kb/d
Downgraded from Bullish to a split call. A deal announcement should be accompanied by rising transits and a softening physical backwardation — today delivered the opposite on both counts. This reads less like resolution and more like a market front-running a headline that logistics have not yet confirmed. Fade the flat-price move; respect the physical structure.
HSFO 380cst — Upgraded to Extreme
FOB Sing $542.16/mt (+$19.54) · Cash diff +$15.58/mt
Extremely Bullish Conviction: Very High
Outright Rose Against a Crashing Crude Tape
While Brent and WTI both fell >5%, Singapore 380cst HSFO rose $19.54/mt. The Aug-Sep swap spread widened to a $23.25/mt backwardation (+$1.25/mt) and the 180cst spread stayed wide. The cash differential firmed to +$15.58/mt at the Aug 3 Asian close, up $2.25/mt from Jul 31 — last higher on June 12 at +$27.26/mt.
The Hi-5 Spread Hit a 33-Month High
The Singapore 0.5% marine fuel vs HSFO cash spread (Hi-5) reached $225.93/mt on Aug 3, up ~34.5% w/w and the highest since Nov 1, 2023 ($230.42/mt). The wide spread normally incentivizes blending mid-sulfur cutters into LSFO, but a scarce supply of sulfur-cutters is limiting how much the Singapore LSFO pool can digest — the constraint is structural, not just pricing.
MOC Volumes at a 5-Month High
Singapore fuel oil derivatives MOC volume rose 35.4% MoM and YoY to 19.3mn bbl in July, a 5-month high. HSFO swaps alone rose 25.9% MoM, 49.3% YoY to 15.27mn bbl; the benchmark 380cst grade made up 68.8% of that at 10.509mn bbl, up 7.5% MoM. Busan HSFO demand surged — "sales reached almost three times the volume of recent days" — delivered assessed +$20 to $679/mt, a $136.84/mt premium over FOB Singapore.
Outright +$19.54/mt Against Crashing Crude Hi-5 Spread $225.93/mt, Highest Since Nov 2023 MOC Volume +35.4% to 5-Month High Busan Demand 3x Normal Volume
Upgraded from Bullish to Extremely Bullish. Every pillar from 30 July strengthened rather than faded, and the product's refusal to follow crude down today is itself the strongest evidence — this is a supply-side dislocation trading independently of the flat-price complex.

Petrochemicals — Upgraded from Extreme
NE Asia Ethylene-Naphtha $234.875/mt (widest since 29 May)
Bearish Conviction: Medium
The Spread That Flipped the Call
The CFR Northeast Asia ethylene vs C+F Japan naphtha price spread widened to $234.875/mt on Aug 4, the widest reading since May 29 — a meaningful improvement from the ~$98/mt reading (against a $250/mt integrated breakeven) that anchored the 30 July "Extremely Bearish, highest conviction in the brief" call. Feedstock Diversification Is Actually Happening
The US Gulf Coast is loading nearly 133,000 b/d of naphtha to Japan month-to-date in August — on pace for the highest volume to the country in at least five years — explicitly linked by CERA analysts to "petrochemical feedstock diversification amid Middle East geopolitical risk." US waterborne ethane exports more than doubled week over week to 1.1mn b/d in the week to July 31, with Far East demand at 641,000 b/d and India flows at 371,000 b/d.
Ethane Itself Is Getting Cheaper, Widening the Feedstock Gap
US ethane at Mont Belvieu slid 0.625¢ to 23.375¢/gal, the lowest since July 21, even as export volumes surge — a supply glut relative to export capacity that keeps the ethane-vs-naphtha cracking economics tilted toward substitution, the same structural theme (LPG/ethane cheaper than naphtha) flagged 30 July.
