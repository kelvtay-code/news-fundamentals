🛣️ Gulf pipelines — 7.3 million b/d by 2028
AGAINST MY POSITION
  • Oilgram's lead: "Gulf states accelerate pipeline projects to reduce Hormuz reliance". The subheads read "Diversification shifts risk, creates new chokepoints", "Goldman sees capacity additions hitting 7.3 mil b/d by 2028" and "Qatar rejects pipelines, maintains Hormuz route".
  • "The Iran war is accelerating a redesign of Middle East oil export infrastructure as Gulf producers expand routes that reduce tanker exposure to the Strait of Hormuz, but funding, political and security hurdles remain."
  • A pipeline that bypasses Hormuz removes a tanker voyage. My freight position collects tonnage absorption from exactly those voyages. So 7.3 million b/d of new pipeline capacity is the mechanism of my position running in reverse.
  • Three limits sit in the same article, and I state them because they are in the source, not because they comfort me:
    • "The pipelines only address crude oil, not, mostly, refined products, and not at all LNG, or the many other commodities".
    • Qatar rejects pipelines and keeps the Hormuz route.
    • "the recent Houthi blockade on Saudi shipping has displayed how such a rerouting strategy could be further complicated by security risks", with the militants near the Bab al-Mandab Strait.
  • S&P Global CERA wrote in an August note that "politics, and security remain binding constraints, exposing a deep vulnerability in Middle East oil logistics".
💡 The date matters more than the number. 2028 is two years out, and my position marks daily. A pipeline programme does not move a demurrage rate this quarter. But I have recorded "years, not months" from a named broker as the bullish case for this position since 12 October. I now have a dated counter-case on the same horizon, and I will not quote only the half that suits the book. Pipelines carry crude and not product. My position sits on the dirty leg, which is crude. That is the part of the story that points at me.
