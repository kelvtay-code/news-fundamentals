Overall Market Summary
Start with the number nobody quotes: the Brent/Dubai spread. The Platts weekly Pricescore for the week ending 14 August has Dated Brent at $92.72/b, up $4.83 on the prior week — and Dubai first-month at $88.61, up $8.85. That collapses Brent/Dubai from $8.13 to $4.11 in five sessions. WTI Cushing rose $5.09 to $82.45, ANS $9.19 to $85.96, Mars $6.59 to $81.99. Every sour and medium grade outran the sweet benchmark. That is not a risk-premium story — a generalised war premium lifts the whole complex evenly. It is a grade availability story, and it says the barrel that cannot get out of the Gulf is the sour one.

Which is exactly what the chokepoint data now describes. Hormuz transits recovered to 14 ships on 17 August from a revised 2 on 16 August (itself revised up from an all-time-record 1) — but the composition is the tell: of those 14, only one VLCC, three product tankers and two LPG carriers, split evenly seven in and seven out, and no LNG tanker has crossed for 16 days, matching the longest stretch since 28 July. Crossings of the US naval blockade line rose to 52 from 37, with the blockade described as "largely effective, with only four non-compliant vessels detected." And the traffic that is moving is moving strangely: the VLCC Greece Prosperity completed a dark in-and-out transit, vanishing in the Gulf of Oman on 2 August and reappearing at Fujairah on 17 August with 1.3mn barrels of Basrah Medium, immediately STS-ing to Yuan Rui Yang. The Grand Lady surfaced at Fujairah with 2mn barrels of Basrah Medium loaded in mid-July. Two Sinokor tankers reappeared off Sri Lanka after an estimated 10 August dark transit. Barrels are crossing, but on month-long journeys with AIS gaps and ship-to-ship laundering. That is a freight-cost and timing problem, and it prices as sour tightness. The freight board proves it: Mongolia Prosperity fixed 270kt PG–East at w570 against Aristotelis II at w185 for Fujairah–East — a threefold war-risk penalty for loading inside the strait rather than outside it.

Against this, the single largest bearish force in the package is Chinese exports — and it lands on one product. China's July clean product exports rose 86.1% month on month to 2.55mn mt, a four-month high, and August is guided at 3.52–3.68mn mt. Within that, jet exports hit 1.32mn mt (+41.9% m/m, a four-month high) and August jet quota allocation is ~2.2mn mt, above the ~2.0mn initially expected. Gasoil rose 88.4% to an eleven-month high of 810,000mt, gasoline 320% to 420,000mt. The year-on-year picture is still brutal — clean exports −31.5% y/y, Jan–Jul −29.1%, and CERA sees full-year 2026 down ~28% — but the marginal barrel this month is Chinese, and it is disproportionately kerosene.

That is why the middle of the barrel has split down the middle. Asian jet fell for a third straight session — the cash differential −23¢ to +85¢/b, a two-month low (last lower on 19 June at 74¢), the Sep/Oct swap spread narrowed to +$2.30/b from +$2.76 and was pegged at +$2.20 intraday, and the regrade weakened to −$7.09/b balance-August and −$3.15/b September from −$6.38 and −$2.28. Jet is losing to gasoil at an accelerating rate. Meanwhile gasoil's own structure widened — Sep/Oct to $6.60/b from $6.05 — and the assessed cargo curve is forward-loaded (front +$4.30, middle +$4.76, back +$5.82). Within the same barrel, in the same region, on the same day: kerosene structure flattening, gasoil structure steepening. The regrade is the trade.

A refining system that is de-specifying its own gasoline pool and rationing its aromatics is not one that is about to re-export distillate.

◆ The Russian Aromatics Squeeze — a Gasoline Story Wearing a Petchem Costume
Kommersant reports a Russian government committee is considering banning exports of benzene, phenol, toluene and xylenes — because domestic output has fallen on unplanned refinery repairs and because these molecules are needed as octane boosters in the gasoline pool.

The production numbers explain the urgency. Last year Russia produced 1.3mn mt of benzene (−10.3% y/y), 361,000 mt of toluene (−6.8%) and 370,000 mt of xylenes (−17.3%). Simultaneously, the relaxation of environmental standards raised the permitted aromatics share of gasoline from 35% under Euro-5 to 42%. Output down double digits, permitted consumption per litre up seven percentage points, and a domestic fuel crisis to feed.

Kommersant's own sources concede the direct export impact is small — high domestic prices have "in effect almost halted export flows, which have never been very high." That candour is what makes the item valuable: the ban itself is not the signal; the reason for it is. Russia is (i) rationing octane molecules, (ii) authorising Euro-2, Euro-3 and Euro-4 gasoline and diesel until 1 July 2027, and (iii) already selling Euro-3, and now Euro-2 and Euro-4, on the St. Petersburg exchange. A refining system that de-specifies its own product pool to keep the pumps supplied has no marginal export barrel — of gasoline, of distillate, or of blendstock. This is a bullish input to the European diesel call and the Asian gasoline call alike.

The one-paragraph version. The Strait of Hormuz partially reopened — 14 transits on 17 August against an all-time-record low of 1 two days earlier — and crude barely moved, settling up 15¢ on the day the US–Iran MOU expired and a vessel was struck exiting the strait. The premium has migrated out of the flat price and into the grade spread: Dubai's weekly average rose $8.85/b against Dated Brent's $4.83, collapsing Brent/Dubai from $8.13 to $4.11, while the freight board charges w570 to load inside the strait against w185 outside it. Sour is scarce; sweet is not; the Atlantic curve is flattening. That single dislocation propagates through the entire barrel and splits it geographically at every level. Asian gasoline is the cleanest call in the package — an offer-less window with a live bid 28¢ above the print and Fujairah light distillates down 25% in a week. Asian jet is its mirror image — a sixth bid-less session, a two-month-low cash differential and a September regrade that deteriorated 87¢ in a day as ~2.2mn mt of Chinese August quota lands. Gasoil is split by geography: Europe up $116/mt on the week on a 37% collapse in Russian loadings, Asia genuinely undecided with a falling cash differential and a widening time spread. Fuel oil ran hardest of all — HSFO +$22/mt in Singapore and +$39.50 in Rotterdam, where holders are simply refusing to sell — collapsing the Hi-5 by $13.07 and vindicating the retirement of the Hi-5-based LSFO test.
