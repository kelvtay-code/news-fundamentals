⚓ Hormuz slows while the Red Sea reopens
DIVERGING
  • Hormuz ship traffic dropped to 16 vessels, from 19 on 20 October.
  • And the Red Sea is moving the other way. Transits remain "much lower than the pre-conflict level of roughly 500", but 69 container ship crossings were observed during the week, "nearly matching the level seen in late 2023".
  • "The canal and northern parts of the Red Sea remain outside of the high-risk areas defined by Lloyd's insurers due to a lack of attacks."
  • In statements published 20 and 21 October, the Suez Canal Authority said officials were discussing increased container services with Hapag-Lloyd and A.P. Moller-Maersk. Canal transit "can reduce fuel consumption and costs in comparison with the Cape of Good Hope route as navigation safety improves".
  • Canadian grades were weak. Pacific Cold Lake and Pacific Dilbit recorded the weakest differentials, at discounts of $24.40 and $25.40 a barrel, on assessments from 16 September to 15 October.
