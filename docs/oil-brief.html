📜 Policy — Russian diesel returns to the US
FIRST CARGO 22 OCT
  • Russia will dispatch its first diesel tanker to the US on 22 October, after sweeping sanctions waivers. It is the first shipment of its kind since January 2022.
  • The vessel is about 40,000 tonnes, medium range, departing the Baltic coast. Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak says further batches follow.
  • Russia targets 500,000 tonnes of US shipments by November, and Novak says full diesel export volumes of about 3.5 million tonnes a month can return by year end.
  • Russia consumes 3.5 to 4 million tonnes of diesel a month and exports about 3.5 million.
💡 This is the kind of policy story that cost me $13.00 on naphtha. A waiver is an announcement, a cargo is a measurement. The first cargo sails tomorrow, so I will wait and watch the ULSD and gasoil differentials rather than position on the headline.
🇸🇬 Policy — Asian distillate politics
THREE ITEMS
  • Japan will subsidise the gap between kerosene import prices, including surcharge and freight, and domestic wholesale prices, from 21 October 2026 to 20 March 2027.
  • Refiners and importers must keep supplying the same monthly volume as the year before to qualify. Japanese kerosene imports fell 42.9% year on year, and Hokkaido retail kerosene hit a record 155.7 yen a litre.
  • South Korea rejected calls for higher diesel exports, but its refiners are working to meet global demand.
  • An unnamed government imposed export restrictions on gasoline, diesel and jet or kerosene on 13 March, with a domestic price ceiling from the same date.
💡 The Japanese kerosene subsidy starts on exactly the session the Singapore jet window traded for the first time, and it pays importers to keep importing. That is a demand-side policy with a volume condition attached, which is stronger than a price signal. It is the best argument for reopening the jet watch, and it is still not a trade until the differential clears +$1.00 with a trade.

🌪️ Weather — Isaias, and the winter switch
SUPPLY SIDE HIT
  • Hurricane Isaias made landfall as a category 2 near Destin, Florida, late on 9 October. At peak it shut in about 1.459 million b/d of crude, or 71.5% of Gulf of Mexico output, and 1.26 billion cubic feet a day of gas, or 59%.
  • Shut-ins ran well into the reporting period. The last BSEE update on 14 October still showed about 10% of Gulf of Mexico output offline.
  • The storm tracked east of the USGC refining belt, so runs fell only 0.4 points to 95.9%. Isaias is a production event, not a refining event.
  • The seasonal switch is now in the prices. Platts began assessing winter-specification CBOB on 21 October, which is the main reason USGC differentials fell.
  • Northern hemisphere heating demand is the other side. Japan's kerosene subsidy runs to 20 March, and US ULS heating oil assessed $458.83 a tonne, down $16.17.
