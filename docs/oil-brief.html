🚨 FINDING ONE — I broke my own new rule on the day I wrote it. Yesterday’s footer added a third discipline in plain language: “a single session of price action is not sufficient grounds to open, flip or upgrade a position — only a dated physical event is.” In the very same brief I then opened a brand-new long European HSFO leg on one session of price action (a $7.50 Rotterdam move) and called the European Hi-5 short the best trade of the week on two sessions of price action. Those were the two largest losers today, roughly $22.50/mt combined. Meanwhile the three positions I simply held — dirty freight, Group III base oils, the LSFO downgrade — all won. Writing a rule is not obeying it, and a rule I can break within one page of writing it is not a rule; it is a sentiment. Enforcement has to be structural.
🚨 FINDING TWO, AND IT IS THE MORE USEFUL ONE — my directional reads were right and my instrument choices were wrong. Compare what I said to what happened:
  • Asian HSFO. I said the cash differential would fall. It fell to $23/mt, a one-month low, exactly as called. But I traded the crack — and the flat price rose $6.24 while the backwardation widened $2.44. Right read, wrong contract.
  • European HSFO. I said the European market was tight and named Hamburg, Gothenburg, Las Palmas and Gibraltar. Rotterdam delivered bunkers rose $10.00 on exactly that tightness, with a trader confirming “HSFO now seems to be tight again.” But I traded the barge, which fell $9.50 — because the tightness is in barging and delivery logistics, not in cargo supply. Right read, wrong contract.
  • The jet regrade. I published the invalidation as “a third consecutive ~50-cent rise in the front loading window.” The front window fell to +64c — so by my own stop the trade was fine — while the regrade itself narrowed 78 cents against me. I set the stop on a correlated instrument instead of the traded one.
Three instances in one session is not bad luck. It is a process defect sitting one layer below the one I found yesterday: yesterday’s fault was when I act; today’s is what I buy when I act.
The correction — structural this time, not verbal. Three mechanical checks rather than three intentions:

1. A hard size cap on price-action entries. Any position opened, flipped or upsized on price action alone is capped at a fraction of unit size and cannot be ranked in the top five. That is enforceable on the page you are reading: if it appears in the top five of Section 10, it must cite a dated physical event, a completed tender realisation, or a named counterparty action. Every entry there has been re-checked against that test.

2. Trade the instrument the evidence describes. If the evidence is a cash differential, trade the differential, not the crack. If it is delivered-bunker tightness, trade the delivered premium, not the barge. If it is a tender realisation, trade the cash differential that tender prices against. Today’s HSFO pair broke this on both legs at once.

3. Stops sit on the traded instrument, without exception. No more invalidations named on correlated markers. The regrade’s stop should have read “the regrade narrows through −$5.00,” which would have closed it a session earlier and cheaper.

And one thing to do more of, not less: when a trigger I named in advance resolves in my favour, take the trade. I published YNCC as the naphtha decider; it printed at +$17–18/mt, the C+F Japan marker rose $8.00 and a cargo cleared — and I was flat, because I had over-corrected into caution. Discipline that only ever removes risk is not discipline. It is paralysis with a rulebook.

Half of the region’s outbound barrels can no longer be attributed to a producer.
The financial squeeze tightened again
  • OFAC sanctioned Golden Global Yatirim Bankasi and two subsidiaries — a Turkish bank Treasury says was established specifically to move Iranian oil revenue from China to Turkey for conversion into cash and gold.
  • Bessent on the campaign: “Financial institutions continue to find out the hard way that we are serious about Operation Economic Outcast.”
  • A week earlier FinCEN moved to revoke US access for Banque Misr’s UAE branches, alleging $1.8 bn processed for 103 companies tied to the Iranian shadow-banking network.
  • Ellen Wald makes the point that matters: a finalised FinCEN rule makes a violation a Patriot Act criminal offence, not a fine after a multi-year process. Rachel Ziemba is more sceptical — the Iran–China nexus already avoids USD markets.
Who is actually still moving through the strait
  • SOMO’s director general: ~70% of oil transiting Hormuz is Iraqi crude from Basrah, routed down the southern Omani lane to avoid sea mines, with the IRGC making occasional threats even there.
  • August loadings: Basrah 2.2–2.3 mn b/d, against Ras Tanura 510,000, Mina Al Ahmadi 375,000 and Al Shaheen 194,000. The UAE moved roughly 4 mn b/d out of Fujairah, outside the strait entirely.
  • SOMO is discounting to attract buyers: the average barrel crossing the strait fetches about $62/b, with monthly averages as high as $79/b since the war began.
  • Three dark tankers resurfaced on Sept 3 after long AIS gaps — Egypt Prosperity off western India, Sea V off Sri Lanka bound for Lianyungang, and ADNOC’s Abu Dhabi-III, one of the vessels Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority named as facing fines or seizure.
Read: the physical story has stopped developing — it has completed. Iranian exports are not falling, they are at zero, and the market has had a month to price it. That is precisely why the ESPO differential gave back 25 cents today after Wednesday’s $4.55 leap: the news is in the price. What is not yet in the price is the second-order consequence — Chinese independent refiners running out of feedstock in late October — and that is the reason to hold the position rather than the reason to add to it.
