Overall Market Summary
This is the cycle where the balance data caught up with the price. October Brent settled 7 cents higher at $88.98/b, ending a five-session run, and the reason is visible in three separate releases published the same day. The EIA reported a 17.42mn-barrel build in US commercial crude to 424.4mn — driven by imports jumping 1.14mn b/d to 7.34mn b/d and exports collapsing 627,000 b/d to 3.06mn b/d, the lowest since November 2025. The IEA cut its 2026 global demand forecast to a 1.6mn b/d contraction, "contracting more than 50% faster than the previous month's forecast," and now expects a 200,000 b/d surplus by Q4. OPEC cut its own growth estimate for a third consecutive month, to just 580,000 b/d, trimming the call on OPEC+ crude to 42.1mn b/d.

The Dubai premium this brief flagged on Tuesday as the cleanest expression of Asian scarcity has already begun unwinding. Where Aug 11 saw five October partials trade with the bid chasing at $88.95–$89.00, Aug 12 produced one trade — Shell to Vitol at $88.00 — with the bid stack down roughly $1.30 to $87.00–$87.70 and Mercuria withdrawing during the extension. Platts had to assess cash Dubai on "notional spread to Dubai futures in the absence of bids, offers or trades demonstrating value." Reinforcing it: ADNOC cut its September Murban OSP by 94 cents to $79.07/b — the first producer price cut of this cycle, against KPC raising its Asian differentials the same day.

Two calls in this brief broke and are corrected in full above rather than quietly restated. Asian naphtha was upgraded to Bullish on three simultaneous buy tenders; one was cancelled and one awarded at a materially lower premium, and C+F Japan gave back most of Monday's gain. LSFO was upgraded on a Hi-5 reversal that reversed straight back. Both are downgraded, and the second carries a process lesson: a one-session trigger is not sufficient basis for a rating change.

What survived intact is the part of the barrel that never depended on flat price. Gasoil's physical structure strengthened even as the curve flattened — the Singapore cash differential rose 20¢ to +$5.07/b, the balance-August/September swap spread widened 39¢, US distillate stocks sit 11.9% below the five-year average at a five-week low, and US diesel exports hit an all-time high of 1.935mn b/d with the USGC ULSD crack averaging $85.53/b month-to-date against $80.19/b in July. Asian HSFO posted a ninth consecutive session of cash-differential gains, and a new buyer — Union International — bid MOPS+$27/mt for 40,000mt across three windows, above PetroChina's standing +$26.

And the risk premium found a new channel: insurance. Five of the world's largest P&I clubs — London P&I, Skuld, Swedish Club, NorthStandard and the Shipowners' Club — are withdrawing war-risk cover for the Bab al-Mandab, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden after their reinsurers cancelled, following the fatal strike on the Tihamah. Cover is normally reinstated at higher rates, but trade can be disrupted during renegotiation. Separately, a US Navy MH-60 fired two Hellfire missiles at the Iran-bound container ship Vela Nova; CENTCOM has now redirected 55 ships, disabled three and boarded two since the blockade began in mid-July, and President Trump stated the US has "total control over the Strait of Hormuz."

🌍 Geopolitics — The Risk Premium Moved Into Insurance
🔺 Five of the world's largest P&I clubs are withdrawing war-risk cover for the Bab al-Mandab, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. London P&I, Skuld, Swedish Club, NorthStandard and the Shipowners' Club all published circulars on Aug 12 terminating fixed-premium war cover in the coming days, after their reinsurers cancelled the underlying agreements following the fatal Tihamah strike. Cover is normally reinstated at higher rates once fresh reinsurance is secured — but seaborne trade can be disrupted during the renegotiation window. This is a mechanical, non-headline transmission channel from violence to physical flow, and it is the single most underpriced item in this cycle.

Militarily the picture escalated on the US side. A US Navy MH-60 helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles at the Panama-flagged container ship Vela Nova, which was heading for Iran in violation of the blockade; the vessel was last seen "not under command." CENTCOM says it has redirected 55 commercial ships, disabled three and boarded two since mid-July. Total blockade-line crossings rose to 60 from 50. President Trump posted that "The U.S.A. has total control over the Strait of Hormuz." Hormuz transits were 16 on Aug 11 against a revised 17 on Aug 10; Bab al-Mandab fell to 36 from 40.

Diplomatically, reports suggest talks among Iran, the US and intermediaries including Pakistan are "moving closer to an agreement to restore normal ship traffic," though Iran maintains a full reopening requires its demands be met. Saudi export routing continues to adapt: July exports 5.7mn b/d (from 5.8mn), Yanbu down 300,000 b/d to 3.8mn on the Houthi blockade, but shipments to Egypt's Ain Sukhna up 67% to 1.4mn b/d and Sidi Kerir re-exports at ~1mn b/d, the highest since September 2025.
