The Iran campaign

Markets spent Friday digesting President Trump's "Economic D-Day" campaign against Iran, announced 19 August. The operative fact for next week is a date: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent holds a press conference on 24 August to detail the plan's implementation — "It is going to work in Iran and we are going to collapse this regime." Two analyst reads frame the risk. Arne Lohmann Rasmussen (GRM): "With the US shifting its focus to economic sanctions rather than military strikes, an imminent agreement seems unlikely. The market will pay particular attention to how far the US is willing to go in imposing sanctions on China." Trump meets Xi on 24 September, and the consensus read is that Washington will not target Iran's oil sales to China before that meeting — which puts a one-month ceiling on the escalation. Barbara Lambrecht (Commerzbank): "Even though Iranian oil exports are already severely hampered by the US naval blockade, the market is now at risk of becoming even tighter."

Russia — the strike tempo did not slow, and it hit a refinery today

Lukoil's Perm refinery (13.1mn mt/yr, 262,000 b/d) came under Ukrainian attack on 21 August and caught fire — confirmed by President Zelenskyy and the Ukrainian military, with Perm Krai governor Dmitry Makhonin acknowledging "one industrial facility" damaged. It is the fourth strike on Perm since end-April (previous: 29 July), the plant was already running at reduced capacity, and it was not offering product on the St Petersburg exchange on 21 August. Ukraine has hit Russian refineries 17 times in August, matching July's total. Russian crude runs were ~3.9mn b/d in July, the lowest for any month since March 2005, per CERA. Separately, Ukraine struck the Taneco refinery in Tatarstan and the Tamanneftegaz LPG/oil transshipment complex in Krasnodar Krai (19.9mn mt/yr, >1mn cu m storage) over 19–20 August.

🔴 The September question that decides European diesel. Russia's producer diesel export ban expires in September. CERA's Tanya Stepanova: "If Ukrainian drone strikes continue at the same intensity, the refining system is likely to remain under pressure. An extension of the diesel export ban cannot be ruled out." A Europe-based trader put the fragility more bluntly: "Any time, it can blow up if there's any other supply problem. Hormuz and Russia at the same time is too much for the market."
Sanctions plumbing — Lukoil's clock extended again

The US Treasury issued a new general licence on 20 August extending Lukoil's asset-sale window to 19 September, from an expiry of 22 August. The Carlyle deal for most of Lukoil International remains subject to OFAC approval, unconfirmed. The UK extended its own waiver on 12 August, to 26 February 2027. Assets in play include Karachaganak and Tengiz, Shah Deniz, West Qurna 2, and stakes in three European refineries totalling 400,000 b/d. Romania has taken emergency control of Petrotel; TotalEnergies now wholly operates Zeeland.
