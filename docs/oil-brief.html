🔴 THE ECONOMICS THAT MAKE THIS WORK — AND THE WARNING INSIDE THEM. Charterers keep paying because the crude discounts still cover it. Basrah Medium was offered at $27/b under the destination benchmark for 1–10 August loadings, $26/b for 11–20 August and $25/b for 21–31 August; Basrah Heavy at $29.80, $28.80 and $27.80. Renewables & SAF — a methodology change lands on 1 September

From 1 September the FOB Straits SAF assessment will reflect CORSIA-certified cargoes — RSB or ISCC CORSIA, both ICAO-recognised — driven by "the impending transition to low-carbon fuels, in anticipation of mandatory CORSIA reporting from 2027." Two specification changes matter commercially: the existing exclusions on PFAD and Category 3 tallow are removed, since both are widely used in Asian HEFA production and are CORSIA-eligible, while palm-oil-derived SAF remains excluded. ISCC EU-certified cargoes will be considered but may be normalised. Prices moved in the direction the change implies: FOB Straits SAF fell $10.00 to $2,487.25 but its premium rose $41.75 to $1,324.50, and FOB China likewise (−$10.00 outright, premium +$41.75 to $1,298.50). American production costs kept climbing after Monday's violent repricing: ETJ with credits USGC +$50.68 to $3,237.00 (after +$812.29 on Monday), ETJ without credits +$45.12 to $2,144.17.

⚠ THE CERTIFICATION CHANGE IS THE STORY, NOT THE PRICE. Removing the PFAD and tallow exclusions widens the eligible Asian feedstock pool at exactly the moment CORSIA reporting becomes mandatory in 2027 — the premium rising $41.75 while the outright fell $10.00 says the market is already repricing certified molecules against uncertified ones. Read alongside Japan's approved SAF mandates (1% FY2030-31 rising to 5% by FY2032-33), Kazakhstan's alcohol-to-jet project and Sinopec's 370,000 mt/yr 2030 target, the constraint on SAF is migrating from production capacity to certification chain-of-custody — which is a very different bottleneck, and one that favours incumbents with audited supply chains.
