3  |  Geopolitics
The important geopolitical story today is not a threat or a denial. It is the Gulf quietly building the infrastructure of a permanently disrupted Hormuz — and a named analyst putting a multi-year clock on it.
🚢 Hormuz becomes structural, not episodic
THE REAL STORY
  • Gulf states are expanding national tanker fleets — Oilgram's lead: "Middle Eastern countries want to own more ships", "high freight rates result in short payback periods", "sovereign cover could complement private insurers."
  • The workaround has become the system. Gulf states use state-owned tankers to shuttle oil to the Gulf of Oman, then discharge onto buyers' ships. Two handlings for one delivery.
  • BRS Shipbrokers, Andrew Wilson: "Iran will continue to exert influence over the Strait of Hormuz across the longer term, so I expect that these shuttling networks will remain in place for years, not months."
  • Hormuz carries 20% of global seaborne oil flows. The conflict began at the end of February. Diplomacy between Iran, Oman and the US has "yielded little concrete results", and Tehran plans to charge insurance and other fees for transit.
  • UAE and Saudi Arabia signed an MOU on rail and energy connectivity — the same impulse by land.
💡 Fleet orders are the most reliable signal in this story, because they are capital commitments rather than statements. A government that buys ships to shuttle its own cargo to the Gulf of Oman has priced the disruption as lasting longer than the payback period on a tanker. That is a multi-year view expressed in steel, and it cannot be withdrawn on Truth Social. This is the only geopolitical item this fortnight that I would take a position on, and I already hold it: the freight leg.
