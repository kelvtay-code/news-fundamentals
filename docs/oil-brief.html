Crude Oil ⚠️ Downgraded
Brent $96.78/b · WTI $89.31/b · Sept Cash Dubai ~$90.40/b
Bullish Conviction: Medium-High
Structure — Reversal Confirmed by a Genuine MOC Cluster
Cash Dubai traded down through a real Done-deal sequence — Vitol sold to Trafigura and Mitsui at $92.10, then Mitsui bought from Unipec at $90.40 to close the window. That's not a notional print; it's a confirmed multi-trade move lower, and Murban's premium to Dubai narrowed $10.83/b to $14.15/b as producer spot offers re-emerged (ADNOC's seventh tender since June, offering all four staple grades).
Disruption Premise — Traffic Data Undercuts the Narrative
Hormuz crossings eased to 18 ships (from 20) and Bab al-Mandab crossings actually rose to 45 (from 44) — traffic simply isn't collapsing the way a "closing chokepoint" story would require, even after the Houthi attack on Layla/Encelia. A rare Sidi Kerir-Ulsan VLCC booking via the Cape of Good Hope shows barrels are rerouting rather than being stranded. The PG-China 270,000mt freight rate did rise 7% WoW to $79/mt (vs a 5-year average of $19.03/mt) — the war-risk premium in shipping is real, just not accelerating as fast as the price spike implied.
Why Not Neutral — The War Hasn't Stopped
US Central Command struck Iran for a 13th consecutive night and Iran retaliated against US assets in Kuwait — this is peace-talk hope layered on top of ongoing active combat, not de-escalation itself. Middle East loadings dipped below 10mn barrels/day (wk ending Jul19) for the first time in six weeks per Japan Weekly Oil Analysis, and government-controlled crude stocks have drawn an estimated 220mn barrels since the war began — genuine supply attrition that a single day's diplomatic headline doesn't reverse.
