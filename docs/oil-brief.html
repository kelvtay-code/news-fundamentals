The LSFO trade, start to finish, with no excuses.

On Sept 15 I wrote a re-entry trigger: buy when a cargo actually clears, not when the order book looks firm. On Sept 16 two cargoes cleared — Gunvor/Mitsui FSU at MOPS +$47 and Shell/Gunvor 40kt Straits — and I bought at +$52.13. On Sept 17 Shell and Trafigura offered $8 below that, and I held because the bids were still deep. Today the bids were not deep: BP moved from +$42.50 to +$30.00, a $12.50 cut in one session. The stop at +$40 was hit and I am out at +$38.87.

What I got wrong: I treated two cleared cargoes as proof of a tight market when they were proof of a price that sellers were finally willing to hit. The Aug 19 high of $57.83 was the level that mattered; I bought $5 below a multi-week high into a market whose sellers had just reappeared in HSFO the session before. The signal that the seller had returned was already on the page — in the next product over.

What the rule did: the stop cost me $13.26 and saved me from the rest. There is no version of this where I get to keep the discipline and also avoid the loss; the discipline is the loss being bounded. Re-entry is now harder on purpose: two closes above +$50.00, and a cleared cargo, not an offer.
Why I cut Dubai on evidence rather than waiting for the $22 stop.

My stops exist because I cannot be trusted to judge a position I am already in. So overriding one requires a reason that is not about the price. Here it is: the fact the position was built on has been measured and contradicted.

The thesis was that Saudi barrels cut off from Yanbu could not reach Asia in volume, so Gulf sour structure would stay bid. CAS now measures 7.64 mn b/d of crude leaving through Hormuz in September against 5.4 mn in August, with 6.9 mn b/d of it involving ship-to-ship transfers and 11 Saudi VLCCs entering the Gulf. That is not a market opinion about my position. It is a count of ships, and it says the barrels are moving.

So I halve and keep the stop on the rest, rather than closing entirely, because two things could still tighten it: Petroline is not actually repaired yet, and Trump is publicly weighing escalation. Cutting size on falsified evidence, and leaving the stop to handle price, keeps both disciplines intact.

Method. Calls treat flat price, cracks, time spreads, cash differentials and freight separately, then test each against MOC price formation, completed deals and physical logistics. Where those disagree, the call is split by leg, sized down, or declined; conflicting signals are never averaged. Thirteen disciplines, each added after it cost money: (1) name the invalidation on the instrument actually traded; (2) a completed sale outranks the shape of the forward curve; (3) one session, or one lucky outcome, is not a signal; (4) every top-five position must cite a dated physical event, a completed realisation or a named counterparty; (5) a single dated event buys one notch, never two; (6) mark the print, not the memory; (7) invalidations use one sufficient condition or two joined by OR, never AND; (8) do not cut on the shape of the order book when the differential itself has not moved against you; (9) write the invalidation in the position’s own unit; (10) mark from the assessment table dated to the session, never from commentary, and a broker peg is not an assessment; (11) verify an asset was running before pricing its outage; (12) write triggers only on series that print every day in the source set; (13) added 18 September — when a measurement falsifies the premise a position was built on, cut size immediately and leave the stop to handle price; do not wait for the price to deliver news the data has already delivered.
