🚨 The single most important structural number in today’s set: of the 4.7 mn b/d that crossed Hormuz in August, 3.7 mn b/d — 79% — was associated with ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman, using shuttle tankers between Gulf terminals and offshore locations near Sohar, Fujairah and Khor Fakkan. The Strait is not functioning as a shipping lane any more; it is functioning as a shuttle relay with a transhipment bottleneck at the far end. That is why freight, not flat price, is where the stress shows first.

Why this is bullish crude and freight

  • CAS explicitly flags the 4.7 mn b/d figure may be revised up because tankers are transiting dark and may be re-spotted later — but the direction of travel is one-way
  • Three Saudi-flagged ships on Aug 31, VLCCs Qamran and Qasba, likely Ras Tanura crude — the exporters that can move are moving, everyone else is queuing
  • Iranian exports of roughly 1.5–2 mn b/d remain effectively at zero under the naval blockade; Chinese independent refiners have lost a regular feedstock
  • Iran demanding ships use the corridor closer to its own shoreline — the Sidr was physically stopped for refusing

What could unwind it fast

  • Iran and Oman said on Aug 25 they were working on a temporary shipping route through Hormuz; if that lands, the $19.71 Murban print mean-reverts violently
  • Trump claims the sea mines “have been completely removed or detonated” — if verified, insurers reprice before physical does
  • The STS workaround is working at 3.7 mn b/d; the market may be paying a closure premium for a partial closure
  • Japan has already proved substitutability: US crude imports hit a record 879,833 b/d in July and Middle East reliance fell to 58.9%, the lowest since 1951
Read: The escalation on Sept 1 was qualitatively different from the previous month. It was not a proxy exchange — CENTCOM struck five named coastal locations, Iran hit a US base in a third country, and a commercial tanker took direct rocket fire while leaving the Strait. The Senegal Prosperity incident is the important one for pricing, because it removes the assumption that the exit leg is safe. That is precisely what the Gulf of Oman STS model depends on. If the shuttle relay itself becomes a target, the 3.7 mn b/d workaround does not degrade gradually — it stops.
🔥 RUSSIA — Ust-Luga Hit, 23 Refinery Strikes in August, Ban Extended to Sept 30
DISTILLATE-BULLISH
  • Ust-Luga struck Sept 1. Leningrad Oblast Governor Drozdenko confirmed damage and fire in the port area; the Ukrainian military said the Novatek facility was damaged, with “key elements of the oil processing unit and technological equipment” hit. Fire extinguished four hours later. Last previous strike: Aug 14.
  • What Ust-Luga actually moves: 216,000 b/d of clean petroleum products and 622,000 b/d of crude in August (S&P Global Commodities at Sea). This is not a marginal terminal.
  • Strike tempo is accelerating: Russian refineries attacked at least 23 times in August, up from 17 in July. Almost daily.
  • Policy response is now structural: diesel export ban extended to end-September; gasoline embargo in force to end-January 2027. Government has authorised retail sale of Euro 2/3/4/5 grades from Sept 1 to June 30, 2027 — a spec downgrade, which is what you do when you are physically short.
  • Import dependency is visible: Belarus supplied ~290,000 mt of gasoline to Russia Jan–May, then over 570,000 mt from June to Aug 26. Peskov says deliveries are “at maximum.” Kazakhstan’s Kondensat refinery will now process Russian crude and send ~70% of output back to Russia.
  • Traceability regime: from Sept 1, off-exchange product sales of 1 mt or more must be registered with the FAS — voluntary now, mandatory from March 1, 2027.
⚠️ A country that is importing gasoline from Belarus at maximum capacity, downgrading its own fuel specs, banning diesel exports, and routing crude to a Kazakh refinery to get products back is not running a temporary disruption. Darrell Fletcher of Bannockburn put it plainly: “The supply situation in products is getting dire.” Cal-2027 up 5% MoM with backwardation narrowing is the market pricing a multi-year, not multi-week, problem.
🇺🇸🇻🇪 VENEZUELA — $100 Billion, 100-Year Concessions, and Washington’s 35%
MEDIUM-TERM BEARISH

The deal, as disclosed

  • North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP) to invest ~$100 bn and lift output above 1 mn b/d
  • US government gets rights to a 35% stake, veto over board appointments, a US-citizen board majority, and right of first refusal over the remaining 80% of production
  • Preferential access to buy 20% of production at cost — earmarked for SPR replenishment and military needs
  • 17 projects under 100-year concessions, 14 newly awarded including assets previously run by Chinese and Russian companies
  • NABEP already went from ~18,000 b/d to 200,000+ b/d in two years on ~$1 bn of its own capital
  • White House: ~$200 bn in royalties/taxes over the first 25 years; US “majority control of more than 65 bn barrels” vs domestic proven reserves of ~46 bn

Why it does not fix 2026

  • August actual output was 1.213 mn b/d, up just 3,000 b/d MoM. Orinoco Belt fell 5,000 b/d to 685,000 on diluent, power and storage constraints
  • Boyacá block produced zero for January–August
  • PDVSA's own target is 1.37 mn b/d by December — that is 157,000 b/d away, not 800,000
  • Chevron close to a separate deal, guiding +50% by end-2028 — a 2028 number, not a 2026 one
  • Keo Energy investing only $90–150 mn over 10 months; PetroUrdaneta produced 1,870 b/d in August
Read: This is a genuine long-dated bearish supply event and a near-zero 2026 event. Heavy sour barrels aimed at the US Gulf and the SPR do nothing for a Dubai market that is short light-medium sour into Asia right now. The correct trade is not to sell crude on this — it is to note that the Brent–Dubai and the WTI Midland–Murban structures have a 2027–28 fade embedded that today's forward curve does not price.
