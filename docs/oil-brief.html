🚨 The line was crossed on Aug 31 and confirmed on Sept 2. Bahri stated that two Filipino seafarers were killed aboard the VLCC Sidr. The Senegal Prosperity, hit by three rockets and loaded with Saudi crude, is listing in the strait and not under command after crew evacuation, per the US-led Joint Maritime Information Center. The IRGC said on Sept 2 that two oil tankers were on fire in the strait. Mehdi Mohammadi, adviser to Iran’s parliament speaker, said on Instagram: “Iran will no longer take into account some reservations like the environmental ones in targeting the violating oil tankers … more than 10 big vessels were violating our protocols and attempted to pass through the southern route of the strait, we hit all of them.”

Why the risk is now higher, not lower

  • S&P Global Energy Horizons’ Fotios Katsoulas: “The Strait of Hormuz has entered its most volatile phase” since the war began in late February
  • The Omani STS relay — carrying 3.7 of 4.7 mn b/d — is “facing severe bottlenecks”; brokers are “increasingly reluctant to finalize fixtures for ship-to-ship transfers” because the strikes hit inside the Omani transit lane
  • Marsh’s Marcus Baker: war-risk premium set to rise above 8–12% of hull value. “It’s about as bad as it can be.”
  • Trump, late Sept 1: “I couldn’t care less if they sign a worthless agreement. I like our position now much better, with almost total control of the Hormuz Strait, and their economy totally collapsing.” A ceasefire is off the table
  • Rebecca Babin, CIBC: the question is “whether Saudi Arabia and the UAE remain willing to take the risk associated with moving barrels, including through ship-to-ship transfers”

Why the flat price refused to pay for it

  • Transits actually rose to 13 on Sept 1, with Saudi-linked movements including two VLCCs, an LR2 and an MR
  • Blockade-line traffic nearly doubled to 47 ships from 27
  • US Energy Secretary Wright claims 17 million barrels moved through on Aug 31, the highest since the war began; ING notes that with bypass volumes “it suggests Persian Gulf oil flows are above pre-war levels”
  • ING caveats it properly: “these numbers are uncertain … it makes more sense to look at average flows over longer periods” — and the August average was 16 ships/day vs 28 in July
  • Brent added 98 cents on the day. That is the market’s verdict
Read: There is a genuine and widening gap between the security situation, which deteriorated sharply, and the flow data, which improved. Both are true: barrels are getting out, but the people and hulls moving them are being killed and damaged, and 57 blacklisted ships plus 150 idling vessels off Oman say the queue is lengthening even as throughput holds. That combination has one clean expression — freight — and freight printed an all-time record. It has a second, weaker expression in the sour crude differential, and almost none in flat price. Trade it in that order.

  • The ban architecture, precisely: producers barred from exporting diesel, marine fuel and gasoil to Sept 30; non-producers barred until end-January 2027; gasoline exports banned until end-January.
  • Where the residual flow goes: Turkey took ~240,000 mt in August (vs 185,000 in July but 682,000 in June); Egypt ~30,000 mt and Taiwan ~58,000 mt in the week to Aug 27. Primorsk loaded 37,000 mt, Novorossiysk 30,000 mt (down from 63,000).

Read: The single most under-appreciated number in today’s set is 3.3 mn b/d of Russian primary capacity offline — nearly half the system.
