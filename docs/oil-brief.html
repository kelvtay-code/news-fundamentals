🔍 ESPO post-mortem: the decomposition is exact, and it indicts the instrument, not the view.

The arithmetic. ESPO M1 flat price rose $2.01 to $111.53. The Dubai M2 swap it is assessed against rose $5.16. 2.01 − 5.16 = −3.15. That is the entire move, to the cent — the third time in four sessions this differential has been driven wholly by its denominator.

The rebuild basis I named on 22 September does not survive either. I wrote that if ESPO stopped out on a Dubai move rather than an ESPO sale, the rebuild would be ESPO DES Shandong against Asian Dated Brent. That differential fell $3.75 today, to +$11.21, and DES Shandong against Dubai M1 fell $3.85 to +$17.00. Every ESPO differential fell, because every benchmark rose $5–7 and ESPO rose $2. There is no venue in which this position survived today.

And yet the refinery economics never broke. ESPO’s Singapore crack margin is +$27.98 on $3.49 of freight, essentially unchanged from yesterday’s +$28.10. Its advantage over Mars actually widened back to $9.23/b from $6.92. Arab Heavy is back to −$7.41 and Arab Medium to −$4.52 — Gulf sour is uneconomic in Singapore again.

So the commercial thesis was right and the instrument was wrong. Chinese refiners still make $28 a barrel on ESPO and lose money on Arab Heavy. What killed the position is that I expressed that view as a spread against the one benchmark in the world that reprices violently on Middle East war headlines. The position is closed at +$14.90 and stays closed — I am not re-entering a structure whose failure mode I now understand and cannot remove. If I want this view back, it has to be expressed against something that is not Dubai.

Cutting to half now, on the evidence, rather than waiting for a price stop — the same discipline that took half the Dubai position off on 18 September and proved worth $3.51 a barrel.
