Overall Market Summary
This was a geopolitical repricing day, not a fundamentals day — but the fundamentals underneath are not uniformly bullish, and that is the trade. Brent settled $4.17/b higher at $87.72/b as the US–Iran Hormuz track collapsed into a mutual-compensation stand-off, with Hormuz transits sliding to 13 ships (lowest since Jul 11) after an ADNOC-linked tanker was struck. Every product moved with it. But the products are moving for different reasons and with very different durability.

Distillates are the genuine physical squeeze and the highest-conviction long. Ukraine hit three Russian refineries in three days — Taneco (324k b/d), Ilsky (132k b/d) and Syzran (178k b/d), roughly 634k b/d of nameplate — with a trader putting Russian refining capacity "out by 40%." US distillate stocks fell 3.26mn bbl to 96.84mn, dropping below 100mn against a 110.80mn five-year average, while US ULSD exports set an all-time record 1.884mn b/d. The USGC ULSD crack vs WTI MEH averaged $82.48/b in the week to Aug 7, up 42% from June. "Med is struggling to supply," a European diesel trader said flatly. LSGO's +$108.25/mt move is the largest since Jul 8.

European gasoline is a logistics story wearing a geopolitical costume. ARA gasoline inventories fell 18.8% w/w to 797,000mt — the lowest since 21 Oct 2021 — and the Eurobob cash/M1 differential blew out to $80/mt, up $22.25 in a session, described as record backwardation. The Rhine at Kaub read 16cm and is forecast to hit 4cm by Aug 14, against 40cm where barge traffic normally halts entirely. MTBE hit its highest premium over gasoline since November 2023. This is real, physical and hard to fix quickly — but it is a European prompt problem, not a global gasoline shortage: the NYH RBOB crack is down $18.05/b month over month and USGC CBOB differentials are weakening at 97.5% run rates.

Jet is the clean relative-value short inside a bullish complex. The Jet CIF NWE cargo differential collapsed $29.50/mt in a single session to a $24.50/mt discount to LSGO, with the weekly differential at its lowest since November 2025. US arb cargoes plus Dangote barrels have comprehensively backfilled the lost Persian Gulf supply — "there is no outright supply tightness," a trader said. Jet's flat price is riding gasoil's coattails; its differential is being crushed.

LPG is the weakest link in the barrel and the contrarian call. Prices rose with Brent, but US propane stocks stand at 103.10mn bbl versus 84.74mn a year ago (+21.7%), Gulf Coast alone at 66.69mn vs 50.46mn, US LPG exports slipped to 3.1mn b/d in July from a record 3.5mn in June, NWE propane swaps are in contango (Sep $509.50 → Nov $516) while the rest of the barrel is steeply backwardated, and West Med demand is explicitly "down a bit" with Mohammedia congestion persisting. Only Edmonton butane (+7.83¢, highest since May 19) and Rhine-driven inland logistics offer a bullish leg.

The one thing that should temper every bullish call: CPC loadings recovered to 1.41mn b/d in the week from Aug 3 (from a 163k b/d year-to-date low in late July) after Ukraine pledged to spare non-Russian tankers, and Med traders are already positioning for the market to be "super long." US crude stocks are seen building 6.4mn bbl. The supply side is quietly healing even as the headline risk premium expands.

🏭 Inventory & Logistics
  • • ARA naphtha stocks fell 6.5% w/w to 578,000mt, halting a four-week run of builds and coming off the highest level since Mar 12 recorded on Jul 31. The prior build was itself a Rhine artefact — barrels accumulating at the coast because they could not move inland.

  • • The front-month gasoline–naphtha spread stands at $241/mt, up 50¢ w/w. Naphtha's value is increasingly being set by its role as a gasoline blendstock rather than a petrochemical feedstock.
  • • East–West: CFR Japan vs CIF NWE at $39/mt (down $1), October flat at $38.50/mt — a narrow, unremarkable arb, though the Asian leg is stale given the holiday.
