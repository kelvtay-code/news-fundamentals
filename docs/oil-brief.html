🚨 The mechanism, in one line: the US naval blockade has not stopped Iranian production — it has stranded it. Four-fifths of all Iranian crude afloat is sitting inside the Gulf unable to get out, and the volume within reach of Chinese buyers has fallen 81% in a month. A Shandong trader: “There is almost no new supply of Iranian crudes coming to China for the past few weeks, so independent refiners have to look for other grades like Russian ESPO, or regular crudes from West Africa or South America.”

Why the ESPO bid gets stronger from here

  • Buying has been pulled forward by weeks — the November trading cycle normally begins mid-September, but cargoes are already being discussed and committed before sellers have even marketed them
  • A Dongying refinery source: “The demand for ESPO has obviously outpaced supply … at least half of the demand cannot be met. That will definitely push prices higher than the levels concluded for October cargoes”
  • Some participants are already calling $10/b over ICE Brent DES Shandong
  • The deadline is explicit: “Some independent refiners could run out of feedstocks for production in late October. They need to decide whether to cut runs or scramble for higher-priced alternative feedstocks”
  • This is the same pattern as the October cycle, when cargoes were also secured ahead of the usual window — the behaviour is now repeating and accelerating

What would break it

  • Russian August crude exports fell 12% MoM to 3.837 mn b/d, with Black Sea loadings down 55% to 380,300 b/d on drone attacks at Novorossiysk — less Russian crude, not more
  • But Baltic loadings rose to 1.870 mn b/d, and Novak says output will increase once refinery maintenance completes
  • Deliveries to India collapsed 59% MoM to 810,000 b/d, freeing barrels that could be redirected east
  • Egypt — a transshipment hub — took 935,000 b/d, up 319% MoM: a lot of Russian crude is being laundered through the Mediterranean rather than sold to Asia
  • Urals FOB Primorsk is at a $22.86/b discount to Dated Brent against a five-year average of $20.63 — the western grade is not tight
Trade expression: long ESPO M1 versus Dubai, and long the DES Shandong November premium against ICE Brent. This is the cleanest mechanically-driven trade to appear in four days of this brief: a named, quantified supply removal (31.8 to 6 mn bbl of reachable Iranian crude), a named substitute (ESPO), a named buyer group with a hard deadline (Shandong teapots running dry in late October), and price action that has already begun confirming it (+$4.55 in a session, +$4 on the forward premium in a month). It also passes the test I set in Section 2 — it rests on a physical stock measurement, not one day of price movement. Note that this simultaneously argues against sizing up Dubai: the marginal Chinese barrel is being bid away from the Gulf complex, not into it.

⚠️ The two chokepoints have decoupled. Bab al-Mandab traffic rose more than 20% to 41 vessels with oil-tanker crossings jumping from seven to seventeen, including the COSCO VLCC Xin Long Yang making the Chinese state carrier first known Red Sea transit since July 23. Hormuz went the other way, to 11. Cargo is rerouting west and south rather than waiting at the Gulf.
🚨 Compliance has become optional, and that is a freight story. Three shipping sources told Platts that the Sweden Prosperity, Vadin and Qamran VLCCs all passed Hormuz in early September despite being on the Persian Gulf Strait Authority non-compliant list, risking “fines, seizure, or confiscation.” Sweden Prosperity and Vadin have not transmitted signals for nine and eighteen days. The tanker Diyyinah-I, carrying ADNOC cargo for an STS at Sohar, has not been seen in 56 days. The Turkish Suezmax Ottoman Equity crossed inbound with no visible signal. A market where the safest ships are the ones that cannot be seen is a market where the compliant fleet commands a scarcity premium.

Bullish freight and risk

  • Lloyds CEO Patrick Tiernan: “The quotes kept coming, even when shipowners chose not to take them up because the risks to crews and vessels were simply too great” — insurance is not the binding constraint; willingness is
  • Every Fujairah-origin dirty fixture repriced: Fujairah–Kochi w430, Fujairah–Far East w265/w285, Fujairah–East fuel oil w370
  • Singapore dirty demurrage rose $3,000 to $68,000/day while clean rose only $500
  • The shadow-fleet Suezmax premium (Black Sea–West Coast India) reached $235,856/day, from $98,165 at its Aug 8 launch
  • Asia sourced 75% of its Middle Eastern crude through Hormuz in 2025; by Q3 2026 that had fallen to 10–20% — a wholesale rerouting of the world longest crude trade

Why flat price still refuses to move

  • ING: US officials estimate Hormuz flows near 10 mn b/d; ship trackers say 4–8 mn b/d; ING assumes ~5 mn b/d, putting total Persian Gulf exports at roughly 50% of pre-war — a number the market has now had six months to price
  • Quantification is unreliable because vessels switch off transponders in transit
  • China holds more than 1.2 bn bbl of strategic reserves and can sustain reduced imports through the rest of the year; July crude imports were 8.45 mn b/d, up 1.3 mn MoM though down 2.7 mn YoY
  • Lloyds does not expect the situation “to constitute a capital event”; gross written premiums rose 6.9% to £34.7 bn with a £1.9 bn underwriting profit
Read: the gap between the human and financial cost of this conflict and its effect on flat price is now fully established — 21 dead seafarers and a £1.4 bn insurance loss have produced an 11-cent fall in Brent. The market has decided that roughly half of pre-war Gulf exports is the working assumption and has priced it. What has not been fully priced is the second-order effect: who can physically move the barrels, and what the compliant fleet can charge for it. That remains a freight trade and a grade-differential trade, and today it is most acute in Russian Far East crude, not in the Gulf complex itself.

  • The secondary-unit detail is the one that matters for cracks. Hydrocrackers and upgrading units are what produce the high-value distillate Russia was exporting to Europe and Asia. Striking them does not just cut volume — it changes the slate, pushing whatever still runs toward lower-value output.

Everything bullish in the distillate complex rests on this, and it got stronger today, not weaker.
