1. Method, corrections and my open positions
I give the errors first. A brief that hides its own misses is worth nothing.
Corrections to my earlier editions
CORRECTION — I broke my own basis rule on the gasoil structure. My 2 November brief said the gasoil balance-November to December spread firmed from $1.58/b to $1.80/b, and I called gasoil the only structure to firm. The $1.80/b was a broker peg in midafternoon trading. The $1.58/b was the Asian close of 30 October. Those are two different measurements, and my own rule forbids a comparison across them. The 3 November report gives the true 2 November close at $1.37/b. Measured close against close, the spread fell 21 cents. It did not widen 22 cents. The conclusion I drew from it was wrong.
CORRECTION — Platts restated the naphtha crack. My 2 November brief gave the CFR Japan naphtha crack as $224.15/mt for 2 November, taken from the 2 November report. The 3 November report restates the same date as $222.40/mt, and keeps the same +$20.22/mt change. The level moved $1.75 and the change did not. I record both numbers and I build no series across them.
NOT new data — the ARA jet figure. The 3 November European report headlines "ARA jet stocks fall to 3.03 million barrels in Oct". I already hold that October figure as 380,000 mt. At about 7.9 barrels a metric ton the two agree. This is the same measurement in a second unit. It is not a fresh print, and I do not count it as an inventory update.
The Marketwire change column fails again. It printed −0.030 for Dated Brent. The real move is −$3.290, from 108.485 to 105.195. 2. Bullish and bearish factor board
The board is the request in order. Geopolitics, unplanned outages, tenders, and the two chokepoints.

Bullish factors

  • S&P Global CERA puts 47% of Russian refining offline. Analyst James Bambino: "We estimate nearly 47% of Russia's more than 7 million b/d of crude and condensate refining is now offline, with nearly 17%-19% taken offline in the last few days alone."

  • Russia's Deputy Prime Minister concedes the risk. Alexander Novak said the renewed attacks "could lead to a reduction in supply volumes".

  • Hormuz traffic fell 24% below its October average to 19 transits on 2 November, which the report attributes to continued ship attacks.
  • Asian naphtha holds a three-month high. The C+F Japan differential stands at $58/mt, last higher at $65/mt on 4 August.
  • Tender premiums beat the spot differential. Hanwha TotalEnergies paid a low-$60s/mt premium on the same 30-day basis as the $58/mt assessment.

Bearish factors

  • Heavy fuel oil repriced hard. The HSFO 380 differential fell $17.42/mt and the 380 CST crack fell from $13.92/b to $6.28/b.
  • Air travel demand is shrinking. IATA measured global revenue passenger kilometers down 0.5% year on year in September 2026.
  • Tanker supply threatens the freight boom. S&P Global CERA forecasts 22.6% capacity growth over four years, to 862.98 million dwt by 2029.
  • Two Indian tenders were cancelled. BPCL dropped both its LPG buy tender and its naphtha sell tender.
CAUTION: the board split differently this session. On 2 November every bearish item was a headline and every bullish item was a physical fact. That is no longer true. The bearish side now holds physical facts of its own: a $17.42 reprice in heavy fuel oil, rising Red Sea traffic and more Chinese export supply. The bullish side holds the largest physical fact on the board, which is 47% of Russian refining offline. The complex answered by splitting, and crude fell while distillates rose. I record the split and I do not resolve it.

One regional weak spot: Midwest diesel fell on a local stock build, and the Group 3 ULSD differential weakened 9.75 cents/gal to a 5.25-cent premium to the futures contract.

Supply, infrastructure and the forecasts
PIPELINE WORK

Saudi Aramco is expanding its export routes away from Hormuz. CEO Amin Nasser said on the 3 November earnings call: "Preliminary engineering is underway for an expansion of the East-West pipeline." The energy minister said on 12 October that the kingdom planned to raise the line's capacity from 7 million b/d to up to 10 million b/d within two to three years. Nasser added: "We are also in the process of — we're doing the engineering, actually — other routes in addition to the current routes."

The line matters because it was shut temporarily during the third quarter after aerial attacks on Saudi energy facilities, and was brought back online days after an attack. An expansion is a multi-year answer to a present risk, not a supply response this quarter.

Kazakhstan resumed crude flows through the Atyrau-Samara pipeline, the ministry said, after a Ukrainian drone hit the Samara hub on 2 November and transportation stopped.

Aramco's CEO also told the market that world oil demand will rebound amid lower oil stocks. CAUTION: that is a producer's statement, and I keep it out of any measured series.

Venezuela's preliminary October output edged up to 1.230 million b/d. India's October crude imports hit a record high as refiners stocked up for festival demand and product exports, although analysts said the increase may partly reflect delayed cargoes arriving rather than a change in buying.

12. Freight
The cost that keeps closing the arbitrage, and the supply wave coming at it.
Record rates, locked in, against 22.6% more ships
TWO-SIDED

Freight is named as the binding constraint in three separate product sections this session. The gasoil arbitrage to the West stays "closed" because freight costs remain high. The jet trader named the freight market in the same breath as the bearish supply. Chinese refiners said they would favour domestic crude to avoid high seaborne freight costs.

Owners are locking the rates in. China Merchants Energy Shipping had fixed around two-thirds of its fourth-quarter tanker days, and some 2027 operating days, by 29 October at freight rates sharply above the previous levels. The company said "The freight rates locked in represented a substantial" improvement, and added that it had deployed most of its tanker capacity outside the Strait of Hormuz as ship-to-ship transfer volumes increased.

The supply answer is already ordered. S&P Global Energy CERA forecasts global tanker capacity rising from 703.72 million dwt at the end of 2025 to 743.56 million dwt in 2026, 778.35 million in 2027, 830.61 million in 2028 and 862.98 million dwt in 2029. That is cumulative growth of 22.6% over four years. Some secondhand ship values now sit above newbuilding order prices, and the analysts call the VLCC market one of the clearest inverted structures in the fleet.

The efficiency loss is part of the rate. As Hormuz flows recovered unevenly, increased ship-to-ship transfers cut transportation efficiency and made the shortage of effective tanker capacity more apparent.

17. Window play — mechanics only
This section gives no recommendation. It names the mechanical patterns in the 3 November books and states what would change each read.
Pattern 1 — one seller clears the whole book
BOTH FUEL OIL BOOKS

Chimbusco sold both HSFO cargoes, to Shell and to PetroChina, inside ten minutes. Trafigura sold both marine 0.5% cargoes, to BP and to Vitol. In each book two trades came from one seller.

Two prints from one seller confirm that seller's price. They do not confirm a price that two sellers agree on. The assessment then rests on one firm's willingness to move inventory.

What would change this read: a second independent seller printing in the same book at a comparable level.

Pattern 2 — the trade above the bid stack
PRINTS MARK THE TOP

HSFO traded at MOPS plus $34.00 and plus $33.00. The standing bids were Vitol at plus $30.00 and Mercuria at plus $25.00. Both buyers took an offer rather than lift a bid, so the prints sit above every resting bid.

A trade struck on the offer tells you what a buyer would pay under time pressure. The bid stack tells you where buying stops without it. Here those two numbers are $3 to $9 apart.

What would change this read: a buyer raising a resting bid to the traded level and leaving it there to the close.

Pattern 3 — the bid stack that empties after the print
DEPTH IS NOT DURABLE

In marine 0.5%, Vitol bought at plus $30.00 and then withdrew two further bids at plus $29.00 and plus $30.00. BP bought at plus $29.00 and withdrew its plus $27.00 bid. In HSFO, Chimbusco withdrew its plus $33.00 offer after the trade.

Buyers who fill one requirement and pull the rest were never bidding for volume. The depth that existed before the close did not exist after it.

What would change this read: bids left standing at or near the traded level once a buyer has been filled.

Pattern 4 — the grade mismatch inside one book
NO GAP TO QUOTE

The gasoil book shows a best bid at MOPS minus $3.00 and a best offer at MOPS plus 68 cents. The bids are for 500 ppm and 2500 ppm cargoes. Every offer is 10 ppm. The two sides price different products.

This is the same family of error as a basis mismatch, and it is the error I made on the gasoil structure in my own 2 November brief. A spread needs one grade, one basis and one date range.

What would change this read: a bid and an offer on the same sulfur grade.

Pattern 5 — the seller-only book
TENTH SESSION

Jet has had no Singapore bid for ten sessions. Four sellers posted on 3 November and the assessment printed below all four, at minus $1.21.

A differential with no buyer in the book can fall without a barrel changing hands. The sellers set the ceiling and nothing sets the floor.

What would change this read: one bid, at any level. Any bid restores a two-sided book.

Pattern 6 — linked legs that inflate depth
COUNT ONCE

The marine 0.5% book shows about twelve offer lines from four independent sellers, because Trafigura, Chimbusco and Glencore each posted a three-leg OR chain. The HSFO bid side shows six lines from two buyers for the same reason. The Trafigura gasoil bid shows two lines and is one order.

An OR chain fills once. Counting the lines overstates both supply and demand by more than half.

What would change this read: separate unlinked orders from one firm at different levels.

Pattern 7 — the assessment that moves with no market
CHECK THE SOURCE

The Fujairah marine 0.5% book was empty, and the assessment still fell $3.00 to $146.00. Ex-wharf Fujairah 380 CST fell $7.51 with no Fujairah order behind it. Both are freight netbacks from Singapore, so they report the Singapore market and the freight leg.

The opposite case sat in the same report. The HSFO 180 CST cash premium was carried unchanged at $49.92 explicitly because no competitive bid or offer existed to show otherwise. One number moved without a market and one refused to move without a market.

What would change this read: any bid or offer inside the Fujairah or 180 CST window itself.

Pattern 8 — one buyer takes the whole paper book
CONCENTRATED

In gasoline paper, Freept Asia bought all three December 92 RON clips: two from Onyx at $112.40 and $112.50, and one from Trafigura at $112.55. In marine fuel paper, Hotei sold six of the nine clips printed, to Dare, Theme, Onyx, Era and Xconnect.

One name on one side of every clip means the curve moved on one book's requirement. Onyx and Dare are market makers, so part of this flow is intermediation and not a view.

What would change this read: a second buyer printing at a different level in the same tenor.
