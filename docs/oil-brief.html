Overall Market Summary
Today is a paper-vs-physical story more than anything else. Crude futures rallied hard — Brent +$3.04/b, WTI +$2.07/b — on the back of reports the Iran-Oman draft agreement on Hormuz may specifically exclude US and Israeli vessels, which the market read as evidence against a clean, full normalization rather than for it. That reading is corroborated by the physical tape: Hormuz transits stayed flat at a depressed 15 ships rather than recovering, and Bab al-Mandab cratered to its lowest count of the year after a ship sank and two more were attacked. Working against the rally, though, Dubai's cash premium eased for a second straight session and TOCOM Dubai futures volume hit a record low — a liquidity/physical-tightness signal moving the opposite direction from the headline price. Gasoline and jet both show a genuine split between weakening US/crack-level metrics and firmer Asian tender-clearing evidence — US RBOB-Brent cracks narrowed further even as Asian gasoline swap and physical cracks widened intraday, and the Formosa jet cargo flagged yesterday as a bearish sell tender is confirmed by Platts' own desk to have cleared at a strong premium. Gasoil turns genuinely more constructive: the EFS spread stabilized after two sessions of zigzag, the FOB Singapore crack is running well above its July and June averages, and US ULSD exports hit an all-time weekly high, pulling US distillate stocks to a four-week low. HSFO's bullish case holds but picks up its first real counter-evidence in days — Fujairah heavy distillate stocks posted their first weekly increase in a month, Busan HSFO premiums fell on rising local supply, and a Fujairah-based trader pushed back directly on the "tight" framing, even as the Singapore cash differential and PetroChina's now-confirmed multi-session buying pattern keep the underlying call intact. LPG remains a genuine regional split, with Asia's CFR North Asia benchmark rebounding on two fresh buy tenders even as the Middle East's own FOB Arab Gulf assessment extended a second day of decline.

📋 Tender Activity

Two fresh Asia buy tenders emerged today: Formosa Industries (Ningbo) is seeking propane for Sept 1-30 delivery to Ningbo, tender closing Aug 7; Taiwan's CPC is seeking LPG for Sept 15-30 delivery, tender closing Aug 10. Both are genuine, named demand-side confirmations supporting the Asian CFR rebound.

🏭 Logistics / Supply Signals
  • • West Mediterranean LPG prices rose across both grades, butane leading gains (+$25/mt to $583/mt, propane +75¢/mt to $573.75/mt), supported by a stronger naphtha complex. HELLENiQ Energy ran its three Greek refineries at 103% utilization in Q2 (up from 88% a year earlier) and is considering delaying Thessaloniki maintenance to 2027 to keep capturing elevated margins.
  • • Northwest Europe butane also firmed on the naphtha complex, though Rhine water levels at Kaub remain critically low (around 19-20cm), continuing to restrict barge movements and tighten inland distribution.
  • • US propane arbitrage incentive to Asia narrowed to around 60¢/gal, down from 72¢/gal at end-July but still wider than June's 40¢/gal — a moderating but still-open arb window.
  • • India took delivery of 1.351mn mt of LPG in July, up 6.63% month over month, extending an uptrend that began in April (though still down 34.35% year over year).
