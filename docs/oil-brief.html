📜 Congress moves against the Russian waiver
POLICY RISK
  • Representative Meeks, a Democrat from New York, introduced legislation on 16 October to block Trump's Russian diesel waiver, tighten sanctions on Russian energy, and prohibit future licences for the purchase, sale, delivery or offloading of Russian oil, petroleum products, diesel and natural gas.
  • The measure would also stop the US International Development Finance Corporation from supporting any energy deal owned by a Russian national, whether or not the assets sit in Russia.
  • Oilgram's own futures headline reads "Trump waives sanctions on Russian oil buyers", so the executive branch widened the waiver in the same week that Congress moved to cancel it.
  • ClearView Energy Partners, in a 17 October note: "This comes as little surprise given the president's historical predilection for statutory tariff tools." The order also created a monitoring protocol to decide whether further action is needed, including on the waivers.
💡 The OFAC licence I reported on 13 October runs to 7 April 2027. Six days later a bill exists to cancel it, and the same administration has widened the waiver further. This is the exact shape of the trap my 8 October rule describes. A policy that looked settled on 13 October now has two opposing processes running against each other. I hold no position on it and I will not take one.
