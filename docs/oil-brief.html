Bearish
MOC Window (last 30 min)

No dedicated crude physical MOC block is published in today's set (Marketscan's Asia-Pac crude assessments page carries only outright/differential levels, not a bid/offer/trade log). The governing signal is the futures settle itself: Bunkerwire's Asian bunker MOC captured Sept ICE Brent at $85.29/b (-$4.89/b day over day, -$2.49/mt bunker-equivalent) during the Asia close, and a later Med bunker report shows prompt-month Brent falling a further $4.16/b to $84.21/b. NYMEX Sept WTI (per LPGaswire) settled $3.35/b lower at $79.26/b. This is a broad, one-directional session-long decline (not a two-sided, contested MOC) — a clean bearish tell with no counter-bid support visible anywhere in the data.

Tender Activity

No term or spot crude tenders reported closing today. Indian refiners are described as having "secured crude through September" and are "racing to finalize" Q4 procurement — a demand-side signal of proactive buying rather than panic, consistent with resilience rather than distress buying.

Refinery / Cracker Outages

Unplanned: Libya's NOC halted El Feel (90kb/d, JV with Eni) and trimmed Wafa (~50kb/d oil-equivalent) production the morning of Jul 28 after protesters stormed the Mellitah complex amid power-outage/heat protests — then resumed the same afternoon once the Tripoli government "fully secured" the site. Light sweet Mellitah crude is popular with Spanish/French/Dutch refiners; the disruption was too brief to register as a supply shock, but the pattern (repeated protest target since 2011) is a standing tail risk. Unplanned (weather): Iraq's Basra refinery cut rates to ~250kb/d on high summer temperatures, per South Refineries Co. via Iraqi News Agency — reduces regional crude runs marginally and forced the refiner to import gasoline. Unconfirmed: satellite imagery (Jul 26) showed smoke over Saudi Aramco's 400kb/d Jazan refinery after Houthis claimed a strike on "sensitive Aramco-affiliated facilities" — neither Aramco nor the Saudi energy ministry has confirmed; treated as background risk, not priced.

Infrastructure / Execution Risk

Yanbu-to-Far-East freight has already jumped from $32/mt in early May to $66.87/mt on Jul 28, with Gibson flagging a further move to mid-to-high $9/b-equivalent if diversions persist — and Peninsula warns Cape diversions could require ~1,500mt extra bunker fuel per voyage and more than double tanker operating costs. West-of-Suez ballaster availability is just 74 VLCCs (~10% of the mainstream fleet). This is a graduated, execution-constrained cost shock to logistics, not an instantaneous supply loss — the barrels are still moving, just slower and costlier, which caps (rather than removes) the bullish read from the blockade.

🇮🇷 Geopolitics — US/Iran

Trump confirmed the US halted strikes on Iran after Tehran said "please stop, let's meet," now in its third consecutive day (per LPGaswire) — but warned attacks could resume if ceasefire talks fail. Iran's FM spokesperson Baqaei explicitly denied direct US talks, saying Tehran is "only engaged in talks with Oman regarding the Strait of Hormuz" — a narrower, more fragile process than the market's price reaction implies. A same-day WebSearch check confirms the pause was holding as of Jul 27 with Hormuz traffic still "well below prewar levels" and Oman-mediated Hormuz talks ongoing but unresolved — i.e., the situation had not moved to a firmer resolution as of the most recent external confirmation available. Physical data has not validated the price move: Hormuz traffic fell to 15 ships (from 17) — well below the ~135 ships/day pre-war average — while Bab al-Mandab held at 32 ships, ~75% of pre-conflict levels. 47 million barrels of Iranian crude are now stranded on tankers in the Persian Gulf, up from under 30 million barrels in mid-July — a genuine, growing supply overhang that the price collapse has not reflected. Per the geo-validation rule, this is a case where the MOC/futures reaction (bearish) and physical flow data (still-disrupted, still-bullish-leaning) diverge — the price move should not be read as confirmation the underlying risk has cleared.

Turkey- and Egypt-bound Russian diesel/gasoil loadings both nearly halved week-on-week (Turkey to 137,000mt-equivalent pace, Egypt from zero to 30,000mt) even as the odd India-to-Russia gasoline transshipment (Garnet/Agni via Egypt) shows sanctions-evasion logistics growing more baroque. Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil/marine infrastructure continue to cause domestic fuel shortages — Novak says "some" refineries have returned from maintenance but gave no numbers, i.e., the supply-side stress is ongoing and only partially and vaguely addressed. Ties directly to price: the Mediterranean diesel cargo benchmark hit $1,298.25/mt on Jul 24 — the second-highest since May 1 and nearly double the end-February level (average $992.65 in June, $1,184.40 in May) — and the physical ARA diesel crack was $70.50/b on Jul 24, having spiked to a record $79/b on Jul 16. Europe has imported no Russian oil products since 2023, so it isn't directly hit by the ban, but its options for global replacement barrels have narrowed sharply — a structural, not cyclical, tightening of the ARA distillate pool that underwrites today's Singapore MOC firmness via the broader global diesel balance.
