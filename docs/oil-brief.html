🌍 Hormuz: Traffic Up, Confidence Down
TRANSITS 20 — STILL 17% OF PRE-WAR

Signs of function returning

  • Hormuz transits rebounded to 20 on Aug 26 from 8 on Aug 25 — a four-day high, against a month-to-date average of 18.
  • The Aug 26 crossings included one Aframax, one Suezmax and six product tankers. The Suezmax Lila Marseille carried 1 mn bbl of Basrah Medium out.
  • Fresh prompt spot offerings from ADNOC, SOMO and QatarEnergy initially eased supply concerns.
  • Fujairah bunker demand described as "decent" because "the movement of vessels looks more certain" — a Dubai trader.
  • BP completed ACG maintenance in Azerbaijan ahead of schedule; Kulevi and other feedstock reroutes are settling.

Why the risk premium came straight back

  • White House: "No negotiations are happening right now" (Leavitt, Aug 27) — and this continues until the President sees Iran come to the table meaningfully.
  • Iran says the Strait will not fully reopen until the US fulfils its interim-deal commitments.
  • A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile near the Omani coast Aug 25 and caught fire (UKMTO, reported Aug 27); EOS Risk says it was a Kuwaiti tanker.
  • Nine of the 12 non-Iranian ships that crossed Aug 26 did not transmit location signals. That is not normal commercial traffic — it is evasion.
  • 20 transits still sits against a pre-conflict norm of ~120 crossings/day (CMBT).
  • Bab el-Mandeb crossings fell to 31 from 41; only two vessels had prior Saudi calls.
  • Analyst framing (Global Risk Management): "Will it mean a full re-opening, or will vessels pay a toll? A lot of companies would not be able to pass through because it would be breaking sanctions."
🚨 The cleanest price of the chokepoint anywhere in today's pack. Medium sour Al-Shaheen sold at a $19/b DISCOUNT to Dubai on an FOB basis — i.e. loading inside the Strait — and at a $6/b PREMIUM to Dubai on an STS Fujairah basis, outside it. Analyst read. Yesterday's brief argued the paper market had priced a re-opening the physical market had not delivered. Twenty-four hours later the White House removed the premise and the market repriced violently. The critical nuance is that rising transit counts are not the same as restored access: nine of twelve non-Iranian crossings went dark, a ship was hit, and producers are voluntarily relocating their sales point to the far side of the chokepoint at a $25/b cost. A Singapore crude trader put it exactly right: "Sour market starting to feel tight as cash premiums soared… but a lot of uncertainties lie in whether barrels can exit the Strait of Hormuz."
🔥 The Refining Capacity War — Now the Dominant Structural Driver
DISTILLATE-BULLISH
  • Russia struck Ukraine's ~240,000 b/d Kremenchuk refinery on Aug 27. In the opposite direction, Ukrainian forces hit at least five Russian refineries in the past week with combined capacity above 1.2 million b/d. This is now a mutual campaign against downstream capacity, not upstream.
  • Analyst framing (Global Risk Management): "It's a two-sided sword. Attacks could take more Russian crude off the market, but if Ukraine continues and there are attacks on Europe, it would be a huge risk-off global event."
  • Iran/Kharg: NIOC is rebuilding storage tanks and docks after repeated US/Israeli strikes — some tasks 20% complete, others 30%. Kharg handled ~90% of Iran's exports pre-campaign. Iran is using "strategic capacities outside Iran" and occasionally the Jask port outside the Strait. Oil Minister Paknejad concedes sales "have fallen, but continued."
  • Export controls are now a supply factor in their own right. South Korea's Trade Ministry is capping monthly gasoline, diesel and kerosene exports at or below the same month in 2025. Q2 combined clean exports fell 18.6% y/y to 84.88 mn bbl; Jan–Jul −5% to 226 mn bbl. China's combined Jan–Jul clean exports fell 29% y/y to 14.45 mn mt.
  • Chinese domestic stocks are the constraint, not policy: gasoline 9.156 mn mt and gasoil 12.27 mn mt as of Aug 21 — both the lowest since the beginning of 2023, and falling steadily since late June. A South China refiner: "Oil product stocks in the domestic market are relatively low at the moment, especially for gasoil."
  • Indonesia is quietly adding demand: subsidised diesel distribution forecast to overshoot quota by 9.4%, Pertalite gasoline by 1.7%, 3-kg LPG by 8.5%. August subsidised diesel ran 58,271 kl/day, +15.1%, as the price gap pushed consumers off commercial grades. Domestic crude output is simultaneously ~20,000 b/d short of the 610,000 b/d target (Cepu 124,000 vs 151,000 a year ago; Rokan 133,000 vs 151,000).
  • Europe's bill: von der Leyen says the Iran war has cost the EU over €50 billion in additional energy expense with no additional energy received. EUAs at €82.83/mtCO2e.
⚠️ The offsetting bear risk nobody should ignore: CMB.TECH warns the underlying global oil market still faces "the prospect of a significant surplus, with no sustained damage to key energy infrastructure" — implying supply recovers quickly once tensions ease. China's crude imports already rebounded 18.1% m/m to 8.45 mn b/d in July from a near-decade low, and CEO Saverys notes stockpiles "allow them to be picky on when they decide to import", capping the global price. That is the single most credible bear case in today's pack, and it is a shipowner making it against his own book.
