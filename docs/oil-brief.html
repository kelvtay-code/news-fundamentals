Bull and bear factor board

Hormuz traffic and ship attacks stay high, and Russian refineries take new strikes. Peace hopes after the US midterm elections reduce crude prices and Asian product structure. These two forces point opposite ways.

Bullish
  • Ukraine hit the Afipsky refinery with drones on 6 November. Krasnodar authorities reported a fire. Afipsky is an export-oriented plant near the Black Sea, and it already ran at reduced capacity. Four other refineries took hits in the last week. (EUM)
  • Ship attacks in Hormuz continue. UKMTO verified 11 incidents between 29 October and 4 November, against three in the week before. It reported an explosion on a tanker 16 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman, on 6 November. (PON)
  • The IRGC claimed two tankers hit mines on 6 November. The IRGC said both tankers used an unauthorized route and caught fire. A products tanker, Velos Sapphire, took a hit on 4 November. One crew member died, the 25th seafarer casualty since the war started on 28 February. (PON)
  • Hormuz war risk cover stays very costly. The additional war risk premium reached 12.5% of hull value in recent months. The rate is 0.5% for loadings east of Hormuz. (PON)
  • Most Hormuz ships turn off AIS. 17 of the 26 vessels that moved through the strait on 5 November had interrupted or limited AIS visibility. (PON)
  • Asian buyers doubt a quick return to normal. Feedstock managers said it is far too early to assume full normalization from the midterm results. NYK CEO Takaya Soga said shipping is unlikely to fully recover even with peace. (PON)
  • Concern about a US attack on Iran remains. Platts said naphtha sentiment stayed cautious on concerns that the US may proceed with an attack on Iran. (APAG)
  • Dubai structure recovered above the prewar level. Cash Dubai against same-month futures fell to 30 cents/b on 2 November, under the prewar February average of 92 cents/b. It then firmed for a second session on 6 November, up 27 cents to $1.73/b. Platts reports growing bullishness in the Middle East crude complex. (PON, APAG)
  • Kazakh crude to Schwedt stays off until the end of 2026. The Kazakh ministry plans no exports to Germany in November or December. Russia suspended Druzhba transit at the start of May. The loss hits about 20% of the 230,000 b/d refinery. (PON)
  • Pertamina paid a premium of $400-$440s/mt for LPG. It bought two evenly split cargoes for 16-31 January at that premium to Saudi Aramco contract prices. Arab Gulf LPG prices climbed $38/mt on 6 November. (LPG)
  • Asian buyers issue tenders for crude and fuels. HPCL seeks 1 million barrels of sweet crudes for January. Pertamina seeks 92 RON gasoline for December. PSO seeks 55,000 mt of gasoline. EGPC seeks up to 66,000 mt of gasoil. Mitsui Chemicals seeks 25,000 mt of naphtha. (tender feed)
  • BPCL cancelled a naphtha sell tender. The tender offered 25,000 mt for 19-20 November loading. The cancellation removes supply from the market. The tender feed carries headlines only, so no reason is on record. (tender feed)
  • Dangote re-tenders for December crude. Platts saw eight Nigerian grade bids in the MOC. The bid level rose over $1.70/barrel on average. Spot offers were all but absent. The refinery sought 1-2 million barrel parcels. All bids were withdrawn before the London close. (COM)
  • Singapore middle distillate stocks fell to a two-month low. Stocks dropped 5.55% week over week to 8.03 million barrels. Gasoil imports fell 48.49% to 43,776 mt. Singapore stays a net gasoil exporter. (PON, APAG)
  • European diesel cracks stay very high. Platts assessed the ULSD 10 ppm FOB ARA barge crack at $76.94/barrel on 6 November. The gasoline crack stood at $13.38/b. (EUM)
  • Chinese gasoil exports may fall. Platts reported tight domestic supply and lower domestic margins. A trader said the cause is the tight domestic market and worse export margins, not a policy change. (APAG)
Bearish
  • Democrats won the US midterm elections on 3 November. They took the House and the Senate. Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer and House minority leader Hakeem Jeffries then called on the administration to end the Middle East war. Crude futures weakened significantly after the result. (PON)
  • Crude fell about 5% in the week from the election. NYMEX December WTI ended 6 November at $90.83/b, down nearly 5.5% from 3 November. ICE January Brent ended down 4.5% from 3 November. (COM)
  • Diesel prices stay far below the October average. ICE LSGO stood at $1,364.25/mt on 6 November, against the October average of $1,469.75/mt. Platts called the market subdued. LSGO rose $3.75 on the day, and Platts linked that small move to the election. (EUM)
  • The risk premium may still fall. Platts said markets reassessed the pace of that change after the election. A risk analyst said Trump keeps freedom of action, and no deal with Iran exists. (APAG, COM)
  • Iraqi exports are near the prewar level. October exports rose 19% to 3.543 million b/d, 1% below the February figure of 3.57 million b/d. Ceyhan flow hit 422,000 b/d, the highest since 2022. Iran opened special routes for Iraqi ships. (PON)
  • Hormuz traffic rose to 26 transits. The count was 21 the day before. Three crude tankers entered and one left. (PON)
  • Traffic at the US blockade line hit 76 vessels. That is a record for the current enforcement period. The figure counts traffic at the line, so it shows flow and enforcement together. (PON)
  • Physical flows stayed stronger than expected. CERA's Jan Kalfus said the strait "never really closed completely". He said "We can still get the molecules, but… we pay premiums". (PON)
  • Chinese buyers took Basrah cargoes at a $9-12/b premium to February ICE Brent. Basrah Medium and Basrah Heavy for December arrival sold to two Chinese independents. Ample November-loading Basrah supply stayed on offer. Aramco set the December Arab Light OSP at a $7/b discount to Oman/Dubai. (APAG)
  • Singapore crude imports fell 39.2% week over week. The figure was 402,499 mt over 29 October to 4 November. Platts gives no reason, so weaker Asian buying is an inference. (APAG)
  • Asian fuel oil premiums fell on peace hopes. The 380 CST HSFO cash premium fell for a fourth straight session, to the lowest in nearly two months. Singapore fuel oil inventories rose to a six-week high. (APAG)
  • Marine fuel prices hit a two-month low. The Bunkerworld 0.5% sulfur index fell to $843/mt, down $91/mt week over week. The BW380 index fell to $674/mt, down $131/mt week over week. (PON)
  • European diesel demand stays weak. The 10 ppm diesel CIF Med MOC drew four bids, against an average of eight a day through September. One trader said "demand is still weak". (EUM)
  • BPCL cancelled its LPG buy tender for November and December. The cancellation removes demand. The tender feed carries headlines only, so no reason is on record. (tender feed)
  • Naphtha cracks and spreads weakened. The CFR Japan naphtha cash differential fell to $55/mt from $57/mt. The physical crack fell for a third straight session, to $183.15/mt from $197.525/mt on 4 November. Singapore naphtha imports rose 75.46% week over week. (APAG)
  • Maruzen shuts its Chiba cracker permanently from late November. The naphtha-fed steam cracker makes 490,000 mt/year of ethylene. Lower naphtha demand is the expected result, and that is an inference. (LPG)
  • The non-G7 fleet maintains Russian crude shipments. G7 tankers loaded about 906,000 b/d in October, 24.2% of total exports. The rest moves on other ships, so the supply continues. (PON)
