🚨 SAUDI ARABIA SHUT ITS MAIN ROUTE AROUND HORMUZ. On Sept 11 Riyadh temporarily shut the 7 mn b/d East-West Pipeline (Petroline) after multiple attacks on the system in the Riyadh and Madinah regions. This is the pipeline that carries Eastern Province crude to Yanbu on the Red Sea. Yanbu holds about 25 mn bbl, roughly eight days of export cover at August’s 3 mn b/d (CERA). The same day, the Houthis reached Perim island in Bab el-Mandeb, a day after taking Mokha. Quintex’s Stephen Innes: “Hormuz still constrained at precisely the moment the main Saudi route designed to bypass it has gone offline.”

3  |  Geopolitics — A Tighter Market, and Talk of Ending the War
The physical war got worse and the diplomacy got busier, on the same day. The market is now pricing both at once, and that combination is dangerous for a book that depends on the war continuing.
🚨 Petroline shut, and the Houthis at Perim
BULLISH supply risk
  • On Sept 11 Saudi Arabia temporarily shut its 7 mn b/d East-West Pipeline as a precaution after multiple attacks in the Riyadh and Madinah regions. It hasn’t said whether any sections are running again. US CENTCOM’s Admiral Cooper met MBS in Jeddah on Sept 14.
  • Petroline is how Saudi crude avoids Hormuz: August loadings were 2.8 mn b/d from Yanbu plus 1.9 mn b/d via SUMED–Sidi Kerir. Yanbu storage of about 25 mn bbl covers about eight days (CERA). TD Securities: if repairs run past a week, “the market would tighten materially.”
  • The Houthis took Mokha on Sept 10 and Perim island in Bab el-Mandeb on Sept 11. Bab al-Mandab transits were still 44 on Sept 13, the most since Aug 18, and 263 for the week.
  • Hormuz: UKMTO reported a ship on fire after an attack on Sept 12. The Indian-crewed MR El Gaia was hit off Oman with one sailor missing. Iran’s PGSA added 20 ships to its “non-compliant” list, taking it to 77, including Chinese, UAE, Saudi and Kuwaiti operators.
  • Poland’s Orlen says its supplies are not disrupted by the Petroline shutdown, at least so far.
What it means for prices. Saudi Arabia has been rebuilding exports around Hormuz for six months, and that plan now has its main pipeline shut and a hostile force at both ends of the Red Sea route. If the shutdown lasts beyond Yanbu’s eight days, sour crude and HSFO would tighten again, and those are my two largest positions.
⚖️ Three de-escalation signals, all on the same day
BEARISH risk premium
  • Iran: Trump says the US is “open to the concept of a deal”, but that he will “determine whether or not the USA will choose to engage.” Separately, an Iran–Gulf meeting on a temporary Hormuz shipping lane was postponed “in the interests of consensus”, according to Oman.
  • Russia–Ukraine: Trump says the two sides agreed not to strike each other’s energy facilities. Zelenskyy’s acceptance is conditional on a “genuine commitment from Russia”, and the Kremlin wouldn’t answer. Trump blames Ukrainian refinery strikes for the global diesel shortage. About half of Russia’s primary refining capacity was offline at the end of August.
  • Oman’s minister: “The Strait of Hormuz will be open — if not today, tomorrow, if not tomorrow, next year.” He added that high prices are “not sustainable for anybody.”
  • Flows are recovering at the margin: 135 Hormuz transits last week versus 96. Bahri VLCCs Majra and Kassab left the Gulf loaded. Truck deliveries now carry more than 95% of small bunker parcels from Hamriyah to Fujairah, avoiding the Strait.
  • CERA: Middle East LPG plants reach about 60% utilisation by year-end, and Gulf supply stays 10–15% below pre-war for a year or more.
🚨 WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE BOOK. A Russia–Ukraine energy truce would hit European diesel, which would have hurt the gasoil spread I closed. An Iran deal would hit HSFO, Dubai and propane together. Neither is confirmed. But Brent fell $1.95 over two sessions in which a 7 mn b/d pipeline shut. When bullish news can’t lift the price, the market is telling me something. I’m acting on that by trimming the largest exposure rather than waiting for confirmation.
🇨🇳 China: fewer Iranian barrels, and bidding for everything else
BULLISH non-Gulf crude
  • A Qingdao trader: “There have been no Iranian crude cargoes available since mid-August.” Iranian Light bids exceed Brent +$20 DES Shandong, up from about +$2 in mid-August, and nobody is offering.
  • ESPO reached Brent +$26 DES Shandong on Sept 11, from about +$12 on Aug 28. Tupi offers are above +$28. Basrah Heavy is offered at +$24, up from +$5–6 in late August.
  • Iranian crude on the water fell to 94.4 mn bbl from 103.63 mn, with only 8.7 mn bbl in the Far East. Traders say only three to five VLCCs are actually available off Malaysia.
  • Beijing capped domestic fuel price increases at 260/250 yuan/mt against a formula result of 435/420, which squeezes teapot margins. Teapots may cut runs from late September. There is also talk of China cutting October product exports.
Two effects pull against each other. China paying up for non-Iranian crude supports ESPO and the sweet grades. But if teapots do cut runs, crude demand falls while product exports shrink. That would be bullish for Asian gasoil and gasoline cracks and bearish for the crude differentials. This is one reason the Singapore gasoil differential stopped falling after six sessions.
🇺🇸 US refineries at maximum, and diesel at records
BULLISH distillates
  • Analysts expect US refinery utilisation of 98.1%, the highest since August 2018, with crude stocks down 1.3 mn bbl and the SPR down to about 282 mn bbl, the lowest since October 1982.
  • The USGC ULSD crack has averaged $102.16/b in September and jet $82.61/b. Distillate stocks are expected 14.8% below the five-year average.
  • Marine gasoil ex-wharf in Houston ($1,465) and New Orleans ($1,525) hit record highs. New York MGO is at $1,630, the highest since May 2022.
  • US middle-distillate exports to Europe are expected at 2.2 mn mt in September, down from a record 3.0 mn in August.
  • US natural gas liquids exports were 3.2 mn b/d; Line 5 is back as of Sept 13. The US butane–propane spread is 48c, the widest since Oct 2024.
