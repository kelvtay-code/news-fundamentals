Macro Alert — A Week of Easing Hormuz Traffic Gets Erased in One Session: Iran Claims It Attacked Two Tankers, Crude Snaps Back, and Three Confirmed Supply Shocks (Jazan, Chinese Export Halt, CPC) Now Sit Underneath the Reversal

The week through 30 July told a de-escalation story: Hormuz transits climbed to a two-week high of 28 ships (from a revised 19) and Bab al-Mandab crossings rose to 46 from 41, pulling Dubai crude down from $97.19/b on 23 Jul to $81.91/b on 30 Jul — a $15.28/b collapse as the market priced observed traffic over headlines. That read broke on 31 Jul: Iran's IRGC said it attacked and halted two tankers transiting Hormuz on an "unsafe and illegal route," turning back four others, even as the US paused its own strikes on Iranian targets. NYMEX September WTI rallied ▲$1.08 to $84.67/b and ICE September Brent ▲$1.09 to $90.12/b. Hormuz transits fell back to just 16 ships on 30 Jul from the revised 30 the prior day — the traffic recovery itself reversed hours before the attack was even claimed. Underneath the flat-price whipsaw, three separate physical-supply confirmations landed today that don't reverse on a ceasefire headline: Saudi Aramco's 400,000 b/d Jazan refinery is now confirmed fully offline since the 25 Jul attack; several Chinese state-owned refiners have formally suspended clean-product exports to foreign destinations for August, shipping only to Hong Kong, Macao and bonded jet fuel; and Middle East VLCCs are now routing around Hormuz via Sidi Kerir and the Suez Canal in the largest numbers yet, with the Saudi-led maritime security alliance drawing initial support from 13 countries.

Crude Oil — Upgraded

Extremely Bullish Conviction: Medium-High
Geopolitics — A Week of De-Escalation Reversed in One Session
Iran's IRGC said it attacked and halted two tankers transiting Hormuz via an "unsafe and illegal route" on 31 Jul, turning back four others, even as the US paused strikes on Iranian targets. That single claim erased a week of easing traffic: Hormuz crossings had climbed to a two-week high of 28 ships (from a revised 19) with the first LNG tankers transiting since 12 Jul, pulling Dubai down $15.28/b from $97.19 (23 Jul) to $81.91 (30 Jul). Hormuz transits then fell to just 16 ships on 30 Jul from a revised 30 — the reversal in traffic predates the attack claim itself. Saudi Arabia is now leading a multinational maritime security alliance drawing initial support from 13 countries.
MOC — Genuine Convergence, Not a One-Way Print
The Sep cash Dubai assessment took into account bids for partials that traded at $82.10/b and offers that traded at $81.80/b toward the close, with four separate Oman cargo declarations on the convergence of Dubai partials (Vitol, Unipec, Mercuria x2) — a liquid, two-way window rather than one dominant buyer absorbing a falling market. Singapore crude imports rose 30.8% WoW to 1.29mn mt over 23-29 Jul, 59% from the Middle East (UAE 665,381mt, Qatar 66,446mt).
Refinery Outages & Rerouting
At least four VLCCs are confirmed diverting to Egypt's Sidi Kerir via the Suez Canal after initially loading at Yanbu, with six or more additional VLCCs believed heading there from the Middle East Gulf or Asia, or diverting from the Gulf of Aden via the Cape of Good Hope. Most VLCCs cannot transit Suez fully laden, requiring partial discharge — adding cost and complexity to every barrel rerouted this way. China's state refinery throughput is expected to keep climbing in August (7.77mn b/d in July vs 7.75mn in June), led by PetroChina.

Upgraded from Bullish to Extremely Bullish, but with a specific caveat attached. The fresh attack claim, the renewed CPC suspension and the record VLCC diversion volumes are real escalations that don't reverse on a single calm session. But the honest read is that this week's dominant move was traffic improving and crude falling $15/b — today's spike is a reversal-of-a-reversal, and conviction is capped at medium-high specifically because the underlying trend it interrupted was constructive, not because the new signals are weak.
