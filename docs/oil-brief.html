Macro Alert — Crude Sells Off Into a Direct US-Iran Exchange; Three War Theatres Now Hitting the Same Barrel; Products Trading a Shortage Crude Is Not

The defining feature of today's tape is a divergence, not a direction. Iran launched ballistic missiles at US bases and Washington retaliated with strikes late on 29 July — and crude fell. September Brent settled −$1.21 to $86.88/b and September WTI −87¢ to $83.59/b, one session after WTI had risen more than 6% on the initial attack. The reason sits in the transit data: Hormuz crossings jumped to a two-week high of 28 ships (from a revised 19), with two LNG tankers transiting for the first time since 12 July. The market is now pricing observed logistics, not headlines — and each failed escalation cycle delivers a smaller price response than the last. That decay in sensitivity is itself the risk: it means the book is under-hedged against a genuinely durable closure. Underneath the flat price, the product complex is telling a completely different story. Roughly 5 mn b/d of global refining capacity is offline, global light-product inventories sit ~130 mn bbl below normal, and NWE diesel cracks are approaching an unprecedented ~$90/b premium to crude. The single cleanest number on the board: FOB Arab Gulf naphtha is assessed at roughly +$58/mt outside the Strait against −$160/mt inside — a $218/mt bifurcation on the same molecule, which is the Hormuz risk premium quantified with no ambiguity whatsoever.

Positioning read-through: The highest-conviction expression is long products against crude — specifically gasoil and gasoline cracks. Crude carries a war premium that demonstrably decays with each failed escalation, while products carry a physical shortage that ~5mn b/d of offline refining cannot resolve inside twelve months. Second is long the regional dislocations: the $218/mt naphtha Hormuz split, the $88.5/mt LSFO East-West spread and the Asia-vs-Atlantic LPG divide are all wider than fundamentals alone justify because they price genuinely unhedgeable transit risk. The clearest short is petrochemical margin. The main risk to all of it is a durable settlement — Mideast production recovered to ~70% of baseline within weeks of the June ceasefire, so the supply response is fast and waiting.
