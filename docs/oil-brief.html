Overall Market Summary
Monday delivered the cleanest statement of the war's central asymmetry yet, and it came from a seller rather than a screen. TotalEnergies' chief executive said crude is moving through the Strait of Hormuz "very quietly, not publicly… in the night, with support" at a premium of about $10/b — a VLCC round trip costs roughly $20 million — and that "crude oil is sold to you at $50–60/b, it's not Brent, because producers are desperate to push their oil on the market." Products are not moving, because they travel on smaller Suezmaxes where the per-barrel freight is prohibitive. The arithmetic he put on it: 3mn b/d of product supply lost from Hormuz and up to 3mn b/d lost to Russian refinery strikes. His conclusion is the thesis of this brief in one line — "a very strange oil market where the crude oil market is completely bearish fundamentally… you have a lack today of products, which is why you have a very bullish product market."

Crude nonetheless held, because the Dubai window repaired the defect that capped Friday's call. ⚑ GEOPOLITICS — the sanctions campaign lands, and Iran answers with a list

The US: "Economic D-Day" becomes an operational programme

Monday's briefing converted the 19 August announcement into action. Treasury sanctioned more than 60 entities, individuals and vessels — including five individuals and 20 companies brokering shadow-fleet ships, running ship-to-ship transfers and providing bunkering, plus the LPG tankers Sifra and G Silver and the crude tankers Quantum Hope, Voyage Elite and Tela. Secondary-sanctions exposure was expanded to cover digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping. Secretary Bessent: "Every country has a defined timeline to shut down activities we have identified. If they do not take action, we will do so unilaterally"; "any entity that facilitates money laundering on behalf of Iran will be removed from the US dollar system. The clock just started ticking." He flagged a further escalation inside the week — "I would expect that you will see a major announcement of a financial institution being sanctioned by the end of this week."

🔴 The asymmetry that actually prices risk. Rystad's Jorge Leon put it precisely: "There may be relatively little additional Iranian oil left for sanctions to remove, but Iran still has considerable capacity to disrupt everybody else's exports." Sanctions from here are close to a spent force on Iranian supply and close to a live one on everyone else's. Bannockburn's Darrell Fletcher read Monday's tape the same way — "the indirect impact here is for China who is [Iran's] main crude buyer — but short of a more direct impasse with them, it seems just more signaling" — and crude duly settled $2.22 lower.
Iran's answer: a named list of 46 ships

Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority published a list of 46 vessels it says violate its Hormuz transit protocols, threatening "fines, seizure, or confiscation" and warning cargo owners against chartering them. Thirteen are crude tankers — twelve of them VLCCs — and ten are LNG carriers. EOS Risk's Martin Kelly: "Several vessels on the list have already been struck in the Strait," and "the list is weighted towards UAE state-linked, Greek-managed and Indian-linked tonnage, with gas carriers notably represented." Saudi Arabia's Bahri-operated Wedyan VLCC appears on it and has already been attacked. Critically for the workaround trade, the authority warned that from 23 August any ship conducting ship-to-ship transfers with a listed vessel is itself added to the list.

🔴 This is an attack on the single mechanism keeping Gulf crude moving. Persian Gulf STS exports ran 1.038mn b/d in May, 4.22mn b/d in June and 2.95mn b/d in July. Ship-to-ship is not a side channel any more — it is the market. Iran has just made participation in it a listable offence, and has attached confiscation risk to the exact UAE-, Greek- and Indian-controlled tonnage that performs it. Watch STS volumes, not transit counts, for the next escalation.
Russia — the fuel crisis enters a second, possibly chronic phase

Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak confirmed on 19 August that "a second phase of market tension is indeed currently unfolding." Finam's Dmitry Baranov: "In some areas, fuel is simply unavailable," with only about one-third of filling stations holding gasoline or diesel. July's daily gasoline shortfall reached 40,000–45,000 mt and September is expected to be worse as harvest diesel demand arrives, with postponed maintenance at several Russian refineries and Belarus' Naftan potentially compounding it. Russian crude runs averaged 3.7mn b/d in July, down 28% from the 2025 average; Delovoy Profile's Vladimir Poklad expects August processing near a multi-year low of ~4mn b/d, with a return to the usual 5.3–5.5mn b/d "unlikely" this year and damaged-refinery recovery taking six months or more. Baranov's framing matters for duration: the crisis "could instead evolve from an acute phase… into chronic volatility and regional imbalances."

⚠ Russia is now a gasoline importer, and the routes are multiplying. The fifth confirmed seaborne gasoline cargo since late July — and the first from Turkey — is the MR Wendrix (200,000 bbl, ex-Mersin), itself under EU, UK, Ukraine and Swiss sanctions. Total seaborne imports now exceed 1mn barrels, previously routed via ship-to-ship near Egypt from India's Vadinar. Gasoline exports are banned to end-January 2027; diesel export restrictions run to end-August with extension possible. Moscow is also weighing an aromatics export ban, citing their heavy use as gasoline blendstock. Seventeen refinery strikes in August, matching July.
