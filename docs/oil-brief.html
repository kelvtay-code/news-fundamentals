A small refinement to Discipline 10, not a new rule.

Yesterday I marked the gasoline crack using a broker peg of $28.45 because the Sept 14 assessment was not yet in the reports I had. Today’s Marketscan gives the actual Sept 14 Platts assessment: $27.87. The broker peg was 58 cents too generous.

This is not a twelfth failure mode, it is the same one: Discipline 10 says mark the assessment table, and a broker peg is not an assessment. It is a mid-session indication from an interested party. I labelled it as a broker number at the time, which is why this is a refinement rather than a correction. From here, a broker peg is context in the deep dive and never the mark in the scorecard. Where no assessment exists yet, the position is marked unmarked, exactly as Dubai was for two sessions.
3  |  Geopolitics — A Truce That Did Not Survive the Night
Yesterday brought three de-escalation headlines. One of them was contradicted within hours, and the other two are unresolved. Meanwhile the physical picture tightened again: the Saudi pipeline is still shut with no agreed timeline, and Libya is threatening force majeure.
💥 The energy truce collapsed on contact
BULLISH distillates
  • Hours after Trump announced the agreement, Ukraine damaged Rosneft’s 178,300 b/d Syzran refinery, with a fire in the tank farm. It was last hit on Aug 8.
  • Russia struck at least two Kyiv filling stations and port and transport infrastructure at Odesa in overnight attacks.
  • The Kremlin’s Peskov calls a truce “a very good idea” and says Russia is “in contact with the Americans”, then attaches conditions: safe navigation for tankers, which “the Kyiv regime is not currently inclined to guarantee”, and the lifting of sanctions.
  • Zelenskyy’s position is unchanged: negotiations are “still at the proposal stage”. No agreement had been reached as of mid-September.
  • Five Russian refineries have been hit since Sept 1 (Ryazan, Saratov, Slavyansk, Taneco, Syzran), down from at least 14 in the first half of August. Russian diesel and gasoil exports halved on the week to 97,000 mt, and Russia is now importing product: 23 seaborne clean cargoes since late July.
  • Russian crude output is 8.8 mn b/d against an OPEC+ quota of 9.887 mn, with runs at a 21-year low of 3.9 mn b/d.
Price read. NYMEX diesel settled at a record $5.2620/gal, up 30 cents. European gasoil 0.1% rose $84–86 and ULSD $88. The market spent Sept 14 pricing a truce and Sept 15 pricing its collapse. For the gasoil spread I am flat, this is supportive, and the EFS widened. It is still $23 from my re-entry.
🚨 Petroline: days or six weeks?
UNRESOLVED
  • US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the pipeline could reopen “as soon as this week.” Market analysts say repairs could take up to six weeks. Saudi Arabia has issued no update since Sept 11.
  • ING: “The risk is that port stocks run out before the pipeline resumes.” Yanbu holds about 25 mn bbl, roughly eight days of cover at August’s 3 mn b/d.
  • The effect is already visible: at least one North Asian buyer is seeing delayed sour crude inflows because of the situation in Saudi Arabia.
  • Japan says it does not need an October reserve release: most October tankers have sailed, private stocks cover 95 days, and it has secured US and UAE barrels. But national reserves fell below the IEA’s 90-day standard to 89 days in July, and METI wants ¥385 bn to buy them back.
  • Japan’s Middle East dependence was 58.9% in July, the lowest since 1951, with record US imports of 27.3 mn bbl.
  • Hormuz transits fell to 17 on Sept 14 from 21; Bab al-Mandab fell to 38 from 44, with no Saudi-linked transits.
⚠️ THIS IS THE BOARD’S BIGGEST UNKNOWN. A restart this week would hit HSFO, Dubai and propane together, and would take some heat out of freight. Six weeks would do the opposite to all four. I cannot handicap it, so I am not sizing on it. What I can do is watch the inside-versus-outside Hormuz price splits, which move before announcements do. Section 8 has them.

Method. Calls treat flat price, cracks, time spreads, cash differentials and freight separately, then test each against MOC price formation, completed deals and physical logistics. Where those disagree, the call is split by leg, sized down, or declined; conflicting signals are never averaged. Twelve disciplines, each added after it cost money: (1) name the invalidation on the instrument actually traded; (2) a completed sale outranks the shape of the forward curve; (3) one session, or one lucky outcome, is not a signal; (4) every top-five position must cite a dated physical event, a completed realisation or a named counterparty; (5) a single dated event buys one notch, never two; (6) mark the print, not the memory; (7) invalidations use one sufficient condition or two joined by OR, never AND; (8) do not cut on the shape of the order book when the differential itself has not moved against you; (9) write the invalidation in the position’s own unit; (10) mark from the assessment table dated to the session, never from commentary — and a broker peg is not an assessment; where no print exists, the position is marked unmarked; (11) verify an asset was running before pricing its outage; (12) write triggers only on series that print every day in the source set, because a trigger you cannot mark tomorrow is not a trigger.
