1  ·  I CORRECT MY 27 OCTOBER READ ON NAPHTHA
Yesterday I wrote that the naphtha demand case broke and that the premium rose anyway. I then said the driver is freight and prompt supply, not petrochemical demand. The 28 October reports show a better answer. I was half right and half wrong.
What the 28 October reports add
CORRECTION
  • Propane lost its discount to naphtha. Platts assessed the front-month CFR North Asia propane to C+F Japan naphtha swap spread at a $11.50/mt PREMIUM on 28 October. It widened from a $6.00/mt premium. This is the second day in a row above parity. A cracker that can take either feed now pays more for propane. It switches back to naphtha.
  • European petrochemical buyers return. The European naphtha report says it plain: “European petrochemical producers are re-emerging as the principal source of naphtha demand as weaker gasoline blending economics and rising LPG prices reshape competition for the feedstock heading into winter.” It also says the earlier state of affairs — weak cracker economics plus cheap LPG — “has begun to reverse”.
  • One Asian cracker RAISES its rate. GS Caltex plans to run its naphtha-fed Yeosu cracker at 80% of capacity in November, up from 75% in October. The unit makes 750,000 mt/year of ethylene. Yesterday I described Asian run cuts as uniform. They are not.
  • What stays true. The premium rose an eighth session, to $52.00. Freight cost is still high. Prompt supply is still short.
The corrected read. Naphtha does not rise because petrochemical margins are good. They are bad. It rises because the other bid disappeared. Gasoline blending weakened. LPG no longer undercuts naphtha. A cracker that wants cheap feed has no cheaper option left. That is a substitution story, not a demand story. My 28 September exit read the direction wrong and my 27 October note read the cause wrong. I record both.
What this costs me. The exit now costs $22.00 against the $30.00 close and the $33.00 entry. The run is $0, $2, $5, $7, $9, $13, $15, $17, $20, $22. The premium sits $19.00 above where I first bought it. Eight rises in a row.
The one turn signal. The MOPJ November/December swaps spread fell again, by $1/mt to $42/mt, after a $4.50 fall the session before. Two falls in two sessions. A prompt spread in decline is the first thing that breaks a prompt-supply squeeze. I watch this number and not the ethylene spread.

Why European diesel rallied
SUPPLY
  • The United States stopped the barrels. The European diesel report gives the cause twice. First: the rally came “with lack of inflows from the US”. Second: “weak trans-Atlantic arbitrage incentives deprive the North of product while increased Indian flows supply the Med”.
  • The US side confirms it. The US Marketscan reports US diesel exports at a 31-week low. Two reports, two regions, one fact. This is the strongest corroborated link in today's set.
  • The North holds its premium. The North to Mediterranean spread stayed at $17/mt, wide, because the shortfall sits in the North and India supplies the South.
  • High sulfur vacuum gasoil gained. Robust diesel cracks pulled HSVGO over LSVGO. European VGO loadings rose 68% in the week to 23 October.
  • CAUTION on one date. The report states the USGC to Europe arbitrage “turned negative for the first time in over a month, at minus $1.84/b” and dates it “Oct. 2”. That date does not fit the sentence, which describes a current condition. I quote the number and flag the date as doubtful rather than restate it as today's figure.

Iran — capacity rises, output falls
DIVERGENCE
  • Masjed Soleiman Oil and Gas Co. added 9,000 b/d of capacity after partial repair of oil transfer pipelines in Khuzestan, the oil ministry news service Shana reported on 28 October. It is the company's second increase in recent days, after four wells added 4,000 b/d on 20 October.
  • IRNA reported on 18 October that Iranian crude production capacity reached 3.715 million b/d in October, up 8.3% from 3.43 million b/d two years earlier.
  • Actual output hit a 2026 low of 2.1 million b/d in September, against about 3.2 million b/d before the US and Israeli attacks of 28 February. That is the Platts OPEC+ production survey.
Capacity is not supply. Iran reports capacity gains of a few thousand barrels a day while its actual output is 1.1 million b/d below its pre-war level. A reader who follows the capacity headlines reads a recovery. The survey reads a collapse. I use the output number.
