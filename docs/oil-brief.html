🌍 Hormuz: From 20 Ships to Three in Five Days
ACUTE — ACTIVE MILITARY EXCHANGE
The Sequence

  • Aug 30: CENTCOM took "limited, precise" action against IRGC minelaying forces near Larak Island, just offshore Bandar Abbas, as they prepared to fire rockets carrying sea mines into the waterway.   • Aug 31: Trump posted an AI-generated video of Iran's export hub Kharg Island "being blown to smithereens." The IRGC navy said a "violating super oil tanker" collided with two sea mines and caught fire on the Strait's southern route, which Iran deems illegal to transit. CENTCOM flatly denied it: "no ships have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz... yet another IRGC attempt to intimidate regional commercial shipping through disinformation." Hormuz transits at midday: THREE — and all three carried compliance warnings for calling at a sanctioned country or port within the last 12 months.

  • Three visible transits, none of them mainstream trade — the compliant fleet has stopped moving entirely

  • Kharg Island — where most Iranian crude is exported — is now under explicit public threat; the US bombed military sites there in March
  • First direct US-Iran military exchange in over a month; ING: "reinforces concerns about a prolonged stalemate"
  • US escalating financial pressure, sanctioning third-party banks described as Iran's "financial lifelines"
  • Trump's Aug 25 all-clear on mines is now directly contradicted by an active minelaying interdiction five days later

Bearish / de-escalation tells

  • StoneX's Mike Castle: "Somewhat surprisingly, [the] Iranian president over the weekend acknowledged the toll that increased US economic pressure was taking" — possibly "a sign of the civilian government potentially becoming more open to negotiations, but the influence of the IRGC cannot be underestimated"
  • Asian buyers surveyed by Platts expect only a modest $2–6/b rise in Aramco's October Arab Light OSP, smaller than the month-to-date Dubai structure gain, "as concerns over supply disruptions continue to ease amid improving oil transit"
  • South Korea's July crude imports fully recovered — 93.18 mn bbl, +9.8% y/y — with Murban, Upper Zakum and Das flowing "smoothly from Fujairah... with few issues"
  • PetroChina's Middle East output has recovered to ~90% of pre-war levels
  • Bannockburn's Darrell Fletcher: the primary US focus "is on economic pressure for now"
The tension to hold in mind: the flow data and the price data are telling opposite stories, and both are correct. South Korea's July imports fully recovered, PetroChina is back to 90%, and refiners are routing Arab Light the long way via Suez–Med–Cape of Good Hope–Indian Ocean — the system has adapted. But adaptation is not resilience: it took one weekend and two missile launchers to take visible transits from 14 to three. The market is not pricing a supply shortfall; it is pricing the option on one. That is why Dubai's cash-futures spread averaged $9.59/b in August against $1.50 in July while Asian refiners simultaneously tell Platts they expect only a modest OSP rise.
🔥 Russia — The Export Ban Is Now the Single Largest Distillate Variable
EXTREMELY BULLISH DISTILLATE
  • Russia extended its export ban on diesel, marine fuel and gasoil for large refiners from Aug 31 to Sept 30, "to maintain stability in the domestic fuel market" (energy ministry, Aug 29). The ban applies to direct producers of these distillates.
  • The full gasoline ban and the diesel ban for non-producers (traders and refiners under 1 mn mt/year) run until Jan 31.
  • Russia shipped just 504,600 mt of diesel/gasoil in August through Aug 30, versus 743,000 mt in July — on track for the lowest monthly total since at least 2016 (S&P Global Commodities at Sea).
  • Latest Ukrainian strikes: Surgutneftegas' 420,000 b/d Kirishi refinery and Slavneft's 300,000 b/d Yanos — 720,000 b/d of capacity hit in the most recent wave alone, on top of the roughly half of national capacity already estimated offline.
  • Domestic diesel supply "has remained relatively stable — unlike gasoline." Gasoline availability has started to tighten again after a fresh wave of drone attacks, with analysts and authorities suggesting the country is entering a second phase of shortages after a brief respite when some refineries restarted at reduced rates.
  • Diesel demand is being pulled higher by the autumn harvest — diesel is the agricultural sector's fuel of choice — though availability has so far been sufficient.
🚨 Do the arithmetic on the export ban. Russia has removed roughly 240,000 mt/month of diesel/gasoil from the seaborne market versus July, and has now committed to keeping it removed through September. Europe replaced it by importing 2.4 mn mt from the US in August against 673,000 mt in July — a 3.6x increase. That transatlantic pull is precisely what the gasoil EFS at −$138.69/mt is measuring, and it is why the US ULSD contract gained 13.70 cents in a single session while RBOB gained 0.79. The US refining system is now the marginal supplier to Europe, and it is being paid accordingly.
🇺🇸🇻🇪 Venezuela — Washington Takes 55% of 65 Billion Barrels
STRUCTURALLY BEARISH HEAVY SOUR — EVENTUALLY
  • A binational US-Venezuela oil agreement targets more than 1.5 mn b/d from 17 strategic fields over 25 years — eight in the Maracaibo Basin, nine in the Orinoco Belt. Interim President Delcy Rodriguez cites $100 bn of investment and $209 bn of tax revenue at a $65/b reference price ("nearly $19 goes directly to our country" per barrel).
  • Trump announced it Aug 28: Washington gets majority control over more than 65 bn barrels of proven reserves. A US official describes 55% effective output of a new private joint venture — "the second-largest private oil company by reserves in the world", split between holding-company equity and at-cost off-take for the US government. Output would "go toward filling the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve and fulfilling the supply needs of our Great US Military."
  • Eight new greenfield Orinoco blocks carry 16% minimum royalties and a 34% income tax — sharply tougher than the 1% royalty / 34% tax of the 1990s liberalisation.
  • Chevron is separately closing a "major" deal in Caracas this week, explicitly "unrelated to initiatives by the US government." Chevron has grown its three JVs from 40,000 b/d to 280,000 b/d and targets +50% by end-2028.
  • ClearView Energy Partners: three possible structures, each with a price tag "in the double-digit billions of dollars," and "little room for bipartisan agreement to this end in an annual appropriations bill." The Atlantic Council's Jason Marczak flags a probable Venezuelan election next year and asks whether deals inked by an interim government will be respected.
Trade implication, and the timeline discipline to apply to it. This is a 2028+ barrel, not a 2026 one — wells "don't come on overnight." What is tradeable now is the second-order effect Platts spells out: additional Venezuelan heavy barrels compete directly with Western Canadian Select into USGC refiners, whose coking margins are already elevated on diesel strength. That reinforces the structural bid for heavy-sour conversion capacity and, as flagged on Aug 28, keeps adding residual fuel yield into the Atlantic basin. It is a slow bearish drip on Atlantic HSFO and a slow bullish drip on USGC coking spreads — not a crude-price event this quarter.
