🔍 ESPO: diagnose the mechanism before you honour the stop.

ESPO M1 fell $2.65 against Dubai. But look at what did not move: Sokol was unchanged at +$23.90. Sakhalin Blend was unchanged at +$17.15. If Russian Far East crude were being sold, all three would have fallen together. They did not.

What moved is the denominator. ESPO is assessed against Dubai M2 futures, and the futures are rising as the physical war premium unwinds — cash Dubai fell to $111.20 while the futures strip firmed, which is exactly what a $3.52 collapse in the cash–futures spread means. ESPO’s own flat price fell $1.62; Dubai futures did the rest.

So: the stop stands, unchanged at +$17.00. I am not moving it and I am not explaining it away — that is what a stop is for, and the ESPO position was sized on the premise that Chinese buying holds the differential up, not on a premise about Dubai futures. But I am recording the diagnosis now, before the event, so that if it stops out tomorrow I can tell the difference between being wrong about ESPO and having chosen a denominator that carries someone else’s risk. Only the second one justifies rebuilding the position on a different basis.
