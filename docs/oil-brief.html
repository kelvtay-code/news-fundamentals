Bull and bear factor board

Geopolitics gave the barrel one fresh attack and one fresh de-escalation signal on the same day. The bear side won, because the attack took no barrels off the market and Hormuz traffic fell to a nine-week low.

Bullish
  • Attackers hit Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline again on 9 November. The Saudi Press Agency reported a "limited fire that was extinguished by emergency response teams" on the 7 million b/d line in the Medina region. (PON, Operations at Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline unaffected by fresh attack: SPA)
  • Ukraine struck six Russian refineries in eight days. The attacks resumed after a two-week break from 16 to 29 October. Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said renewed attacks "could lead to a reduction in supply volumes" against a 2.15 million mt November diesel export target. (PON, Russian diesel exports rise despite refinery strikes)
  • PDVSA's refining system stayed far below capacity. The four-refinery system holds 1.3 million b/d, yet the CRP complex ran at 32.5%. The 105,000 b/d Cardon FCC stayed halted for repairs. El Palito stayed halted for maintenance. (PON, REFINERY NEWS: PDVSA CRP complex rises to 32.5% of capacity)
  • Crude buy tenders stayed open into January. India's HPCL sought indicative offers for 1 million barrels of sweet crudes for January delivery on 6 November. Nigeria's Dangote refinery re-tendered for December arriving crude on the same date. (Tenders list, 11/06/2026)
  • The UKMTO logged its 200th ship incident this year in the Persian Gulf. The latest reports for 7 November involved a bulk carrier, a tanker and military forces. (PON, OIL FUTURES: Crude extends decline as concerns about US-Iran escalation fade)
Bearish
  • Concerns of an imminent US-Iran escalation faded. President Trump met Qatari Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman al-Thani on 9 November. TD Securities said Trump "continues to signal no appetite for further escalation with Iran, keeping downward pressure on crude oil prices". (PON, OIL FUTURES)
  • The pipeline attack took no barrels off the market. Saudi state news reported no impact on operations and no injuries. (PON, Operations at Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline unaffected by fresh attack: SPA)
  • Russian diesel exports rose through the refinery strikes. Weekly flows reached 410,000 mt in the week ended 5 November. The prior week held 358,000 mt. The US took 141,000 mt, up from 36,000 mt. (PON, Russian diesel exports rise despite refinery strikes)
  • India's BPCL cancelled two tenders on the same date. It cancelled the buy tender for evenly-split November and December LPG cargoes on 3 November. It cancelled the offer of 25,000 mt of naphtha for 19-20 November loading on the same date. (Tenders list, 11/03/2026)
  • Hormuz traffic fell to a nine-week low. The strait took 139 transits in the week ended 8 November, the lowest since 125 transits in the week ended 6 September. Bab al-Mandab transits climbed to 35 ships from 30. (PON, OIL FUTURES)

Crude — Neutral (analyst Bearish, skeptic DOWNGRADE)

Headline price: PCAAS00 Dated Brent 91.76 $/b, −4.715 (−4.9%, extreme).

One-source risk No Asian assessment exists. The call rests on Atlantic basin data and futures alone.

Drivers

  • Market Structure (bear). Dated Brent fell 4.715 to 91.76 $/b, a 4.9% move. Brent CFD Week 1 (Feb) fell 3.02 to 0.39. Brent January fell 4.01 to 93.88. (COM key benchmarks PCAAS00, PCAAQ00; COM Dated Brent/BFOE swaps table PCAKA00)
  • Demand (bear). Crude fell as concerns of an imminent US-Iran escalation faded. NYMEX December WTI settled $3.98 lower at $86.85/b. ICE January Brent settled $5.75 lower at $92.57/b. (PON OIL FUTURES)
  • Demand (bear). Hormuz traffic was 139 transits in the week ended 8 November, the lowest since early September. Hormuz volume is weak, so the supply-risk premium lost support. (PON OIL FUTURES)
  • Supply (bear). Traders called the Atlantic basin well supplied with the arbs east closed. Freight and CFD structure both eased. (COM North Sea crude commentary, third trader quote)
  • Supply (bull). The UKMTO logged its 200th ship incident this year in the Persian Gulf. Ukraine struck six Russian refineries in eight days. Neither stopped the sell-off. (PON OIL FUTURES; PON Russian diesel exports rise despite refinery strikes)
  • Modifier (bear). Analysts say further material downside should be limited without a concrete deal. This softens the size of the move. (PON OIL FUTURES, TD Securities quote)
  • Demand (bull). HPCL seeks 1 million barrels of sweet crudes for January. BPCL bought WTI Midland for December-January. Both rows are dated 3 to 6 November, so they are older than this session. (Tenders list)

Analyst rationale

Market Structure, Demand and Supply all point down. The move is a day-one reversal of the 6 November bounce, from 96.475 to 91.76. The analyst therefore held the call one notch lower at Bearish, not Very Bearish. Futures fell more than $5/b in one day, so crude and products are coupled today. Downside is slower without a US-Iran deal, because analysts see limited further downside until one is signed.

Skeptic counter

The physical Dated Brent window contradicts the call. Of the six basket crudes, only Brent Blend and Forties traded in the Market on Close process. Platts assessed both unchanged from 19 November to 8 December, "in the absence of indications testing the Nov. 6 assessment". The whole 4.715 $/b fall is futures and CFD arithmetic, not a physical sell-off. The only trader quote in evidence expects the opposite: "We suspect FOB diffs will rally in the wider market, but more because the costs have gone down than a change in supply-demand [balances]."

The skeptic raised two counts and did not veto, because the three-pillar minimum holds. First, the Supply bear driver inverts its own source. COM presents easing freight and easing CFD structure as the reason "some optimism had emerged regarding a rebound in North Sea crude differentials". Second, the brief did not flag a Platts window divergence. The physical window did not confirm the 4.9% fall. A Bearish call on a day-one reversal with a flat window and no Asian confirmation is one notch too strong.

LPG — Neutral (analyst Neutral, skeptic PASS)

Headline price: PMABB00 Propane FOB NWE Seagoing 876 $/mt, −22.

One-source risk All price drivers come from the LPG report. The tender rows are the only second source.

Drivers

  • Market Structure (bear). Propane FOB NWE Seagoing fell 22 to 876 $/mt. Propane CIF NWE large cargo (PMABA00) fell 22.75 to 775.5. No bids, offers or trades occurred in the MOC, so Platts assessed on trader sentiment. (LPG Europe LPG assessments table PMABB00, PMABA00; LPG Platts European Propane Daily Commentary)
  • Supply (bear). NWE propane supply stayed adequate. Transatlantic arrivals covered prompt needs. Secondary inventories stayed comfortable, which limited buying urgency. (LPG Platts European Propane Daily Commentary)
  • Demand (bear). Flexible steam crackers weighed propane against naphtha. Interest sat in prompt parcels with little appetite for forward positions. Europe continues to rationalize cracking capacity. (LPG Platts European Propane Daily Commentary)
  • Supply (bull). The West Mediterranean LPG market was tight with little spot supply. North African volumes were committed to term. Butane FOB West Med fell 48 to 1135 $/mt but stayed at a premium. (LPG Platts West Mediterranean LPG commentary PMAAM00)
  • Demand (bull). Seasonal cylinder offtake rose in Spain, Italy and Morocco. Distributors built stocks before winter. NWE retail heating offtake improved modestly. (LPG Platts West Mediterranean LPG commentary; LPG Platts European Propane Daily Commentary)
  • Demand (bull). Pertamina bought LPG for 16-31 January on 6 November. PV Gas seeks term LPG for 2027. BPCL cancelled its November-December LPG buy tender on 3 November. (Tenders list)

Analyst rationale

Asia is the primary region, and the desk has insufficient data to form a view there. Europe is split. NWE propane is bearish on adequate supply, no MOC interest and weak crude. West Med is bullish on tight spot supply and seasonal demand. The signals diverge and the Asia pillar is missing, so conviction stays Neutral. The 4.9% Dated Brent fall adds bearish pressure, but it cannot set an Asian LPG call without a CFR North Asia assessment.

Skeptic counter

The bullish West Med pillar is weaker than it reads. FOB West Med propane also fell 22.75 to 958, and the butane that anchors the "tight" call fell 48 in the same session. Tightness therefore did not hold price. On a Neutral call with the Asian pillar absent this changes nothing, because the verdict already records the split rather than a side. The skeptic verified every number and passed the call.
