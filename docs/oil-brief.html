Macro Alert — Crude Craters as Trump Postpones the Iran Strike and OPEC+ Adds Another 188,000 B/D; The Product Complex Refuses to Follow, With ARA Diesel Stocks at a Four-Year Low and MTBE Blending Spreads at a Record

The defining move of the session is a violent flat-price reversal with almost no product-side confirmation. NYMEX September WTI settled −$4.33 to $80.34/b and ICE October Brent −$4.16 to $83.77/b after President Trump called off what he described as "the biggest attack since World War 2" on Iran and said the Strait of Hormuz could be "completely open" within days, with denuclearization talks to follow. OPEC+ voluntary cutters piled on with a fourth consecutive monthly quota increase — 188,000 b/d for September, split mostly between Saudi Arabia (+62,000) and Russia (+62,000) — even as the JMMC formally condemned attacks on energy infrastructure and maritime routes. Hormuz traffic actually firmed to 17 ships on Aug. 2 from 15, but July's full-month tally still fell to 848 crossings from 917 in June, with Iran-linked traffic now 53% of all transits. Underneath that flat-price collapse, the physical complex barely blinked. ARA gasoil and diesel stocks fell 13.5% in a week to a four-year low of 1.415 million mt; the Asian MTBE-to-gasoline-swaps differential hit a record $11.58/b, more than seven times the prior session's level, on genuine blending-component scarcity; and Singapore's LSFO backwardation widened to $64/mt even as HSFO's narrowed — a split verdict that is this cycle's clearest tell on which parts of the barrel are still short and which are simply trading Iran headlines.

Product Analysis — Geopolitics / MOC / Inventories / Tenders / Refinery Outages

Crude Oil — Downgraded
WTI $80.34/b (−$4.33) · Brent $83.77/b (−$4.16) · Cash Dubai $78.21/b
Bearish Conviction: Medium
Geopolitics — The Attack That Didn't Happen
Trump told reporters he postponed "the biggest attack since World War 2" at Tehran's request, framing a two-phase deal: reopening the Strait of Hormuz first, denuclearization second. Iran denies any negotiations beyond bilateral Oman talks on Hormuz transit management. Hormuz traffic firmed to 17 ships on Aug. 2 from 15, though July's full-month total still fell to 848 crossings from 917 in June, with Iran-linked traffic now 53% of transits (up from 42%). The EU's IRINI mission boarded another sanctioned tanker (Toa Payoh) in the Mediterranean, expanding shadow-fleet enforcement toward the Red Sea and Indian Ocean via EUNAVFOR Atalanta.
MOC — Dubai Eases on Thin, One-Sided Trade
October cash Dubai fell $3.59/b to $78.21/b, with its premium to futures narrowing $1.25/b to $4.04/b. Only one trade printed all session: Mercuria bought 25,000 bbl from Vitol at $81.00. Basrah Medium and Heavy crudes were offered at deep discounts for August loading — Medium at $27-25/b under marker, Heavy at $29.80-27.80/b under — a sign Iraqi barrels are struggling to clear even as OPEC+ raises everyone's quota simultaneously.
Tenders & Refinery-Side Supply
OPEC+ added 188,000 b/d for September — Saudi and Russia each +62,000, Iraq +26,000, Kuwait +16,000. India's MRPL is seeking 1-2mn bbl for Oct. 1-7 delivery (closes Aug. 4). Japan's US crude imports hit a record 680,311 b/d in June as refiners diversify away from a Gulf still seen as structurally risky; ENEOS took a second consecutive month of non-sanctioned Sakhalin Blend. ADNOC will move to prompt-month Dubai-linked OSP pricing from Nov. 1, prompting ICE to suspend illiquid Murban futures months.

Downgraded from Extremely Bullish to Bearish (fragile). Every fresh signal today — the stand-down, the quota hike, the Basrah discounts, the thin one-buyer Dubai window — points the same direction, which is unusual for this desk's crude calls. Conviction is capped at medium specifically because this is headline-driven de-escalation, and the identical pattern reversed within three sessions last cycle. Russian refining losses (now ~40% of capacity) are the one supply-side fact that doesn't reverse on a diplomatic headline.
Gasoil / Diesel — Held
Aug-Sep swap spread $9.75/b · Cash diff +3¢ to $6.88/b
Extremely Bullish Conviction: Very High
Inventories — A Four-Year Low
Diesel and gasoil stocks in the Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp hub fell 221,000mt (−13.51%) to 1.415 million mt in the week to July 30 — the lowest level since the week ended July 16, 2022, and down 18.21% year over year. US ULSD inventories, by contrast, rose 940,000 bbl to a four-month high of 100.103 million bbl, underscoring that the shortage is a European, not global, phenomenon.
Geopolitics & Refinery Outages — Russian Losses Keep Growing
Ukraine drone strikes hit Russia's Ufa cluster (Novoil, Ufaneftekhim, Ufa/Ufimski — a combined 464,000 b/d) and the Saratov refinery (135,000 b/d, its 16th attack since 2024) on Aug. 1-2. S&P Global CERA now estimates ~2.78mn b/d of Russian refining capacity offline as of end-July, roughly 40% of total capacity and up ~400,000 b/d from the June estimate.
MOC & Trade Flow — US Diverting Away From Europe
Paper cleared genuinely today: DARE sold 50,000 bbl to UITSG at $142.90/b and HOTEI sold 50,000 bbl to TRAFI at $142.80/b. Physically, BP bought from Unipec at MOPS +$8.00/b for 150,000 bbl (Aug. 18-22). US diesel and gasoil exports to Europe are projected at just 696,000mt in July — down from 1.2mn mt in June and 1mn mt a year ago — as the US instead sent 3.798mn mt to Latin America, up 341,000mt year over year, competing directly for the same USGC barrels.
ARA Stocks 4-Year Low, −13.51% WoW Russian Refining Offline ~40% of Capacity US-to-Europe Exports Halved vs June US ULSD at 4-Month High — the Atlantic Release Valve
Held at Extremely Bullish. This is the one product thesis crude's reversal didn't touch — a genuine European physical shortage confirmed by a four-year-low stock print, widening Russian refinery losses, and a US export pattern actively starving Europe in favor of Latin America. The counterweight remains the same as last cycle: US Gulf Coast length is the pressure valve if the arb ever opens.
Gasoline / RBOB — Held, Physical/Swap Split
Physical crack $29.25-29.30/b · Swaps crack $21.35-21.40/b
Bullish Conviction: Medium-High
Geopolitics & Refinery Outages — China's Export Halt Now Quantified
Chinese gasoline export levels for August are now estimated at just ~20,000mt, against 400,000-500,000mt anticipated for July — a near-total confirmation of the export suspension flagged as a qualitative signal in prior cycles. On the US West Coast, Portland's suboctane differential surged 24¢ to NYMEX +28¢/gal on chatter of a regional pipeline disruption, the highest level since June 2.
MOC — The Blending Component Crisis
The Asian MTBE differential to 92 RON gasoline swaps hit $11.58/b at the July 31 close, up from $1.50/b the prior session and the highest level since the assessment launched in January 2025 — MTBE supply flipped from long in June to short in August. The physical FOB Singapore 92 RON crack was pegged $29.25-29.30/b intraday, sharply above the swaps crack at $21.35-21.40/b (down from $25.16/b at the July 31 close), as the broader crude selloff dragged paper down faster than spot.
Inventories & Trade Flow
Singapore MOC gasoline volumes rose 58.1% month over month to 2.45mn bbl in July, with 92 RON trades up 83.33% to 2.2mn bbl. Vitol was the largest buyer (1.9mn bbl); SK Energy displaced Aramco as the largest seller with 1mn bbl, up tenfold from 100,000 bbl the prior month — a genuine supply-side shift in who is providing barrels to the region.
China August Exports ~20,000mt, Fr. 400-500,000mt MTBE Differential Hits Record $11.58/b Swaps Crack Crashes to $21.35-21.40/b Physical Crack Holds Near $29/b
Held at Bullish; the physical/swap divergence is the story to watch. The Chinese export collapse and record MTBE scarcity are both hard confirmations of a physically tight market. The swaps crack crashing alongside crude, while physical stayed firm, argues that paper overreacted to the Iran headline rather than to any change in the underlying gasoline balance — a gap that should close, and the direction it closes in will determine whether this upgrades or fades.

Jet Kerosene — Held, Conviction Raised
Aug-Sep swap spread +$6.05/b (fr. +$5.19/b)
Bullish Conviction: Medium-High
Geopolitics & MOC — Widening Into a Falling Tape
The balance-month Aug-Sep FOB Singapore jet swap time spread widened to +$6.05/b in midmorning trading, up from the +$5.19/b assessed at the July 31 close — a genuine decoupling, since this move happened while crude was falling $4+. China's finalized August export plan for jet has not yet emerged, sources said, keeping regional supply uncertainty elevated. Strength has also been attributed to firming ICE low-sulfur gasoil futures as low Rhine water levels (28cm at Kaub) threaten inland European distillate supply.
Inventories
Jet fuel and kerosene stocks in the ARA hub rose 5.45% to 542,000mt in the week to July 30, though they remain down about 34% year over year. Singapore MOC jet/kerosene trade volume fell to 175,000 bbl in July from 200,000 bbl in June (versus nil a year earlier) — only one FOB Singapore cargo changed hands all month, Vitol selling to BP.
Demand-Side Headwind
IATA data show Asia-Pacific international jet demand up just 0.4% year over year, with intra-Asia passenger traffic down 2.6% in June — the first decline since the pandemic — as airlines cut short-haul capacity 4.8% in response to elevated fuel costs. Korean Air, JAL and ANA have all raised fuel surcharges for July-August bookings, roughly tripling from earlier-year levels in Korean Air's case.
