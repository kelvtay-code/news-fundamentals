01The edge

Across 18,676 ticker-days the labels that sound bullish lose money. Wyckoff Mark-up, a top-third price position, dealers long gamma and price above the 200-day all produced negative market-relative returns. The repeatable edge runs the other way.

The winning edge: buy capitulation into short dealer gamma — Wyckoff Mark-down or Distribution, bottom third of the 52-week range, GEX ≤ 0 and spot below the gamma flip, ideally on expanding volume with IV Rank ≥ 50. That cohort beat the universe by +0.65% over 5 sessions at a 52% hit rate. Dealers short gamma must buy strength and sell weakness, so once a washed-out name turns their hedging amplifies it rather than pinning it.

05How to trade it, and what would break it

  • Structure. IV Rank ≥ 50 on a washed-out name argues for selling a put credit spread with the short strike below the put wall rather than buying stock — you get paid for the elevated premium the signal itself requires.
  • Hold 5 sessions. The 10-day horizon adds little over the 5-day. Take the bounce; do not marry it.
  • The stop is structural, not a price. The thesis is dealer short gamma. If GEX flips positive or spot reclaims the gamma flip and stalls, the mechanism is gone and the trade is over.
  • One regime. 101 snapshot days across roughly three months of a choppy, mean-reverting tape. In a sustained trending selloff “buy the capitulation” is precisely the rule that bleeds. Treat the negative-momentum finding as regime-dependent, not a law.
  • Overlapping observations. 5-day forward windows sampled daily overlap heavily, so t-statistics overstate significance. Month-by-month consistency and the holdout are the real evidence.
  • Spot moves, not option P&L. No premium, theta, slippage or commissions are modelled.
